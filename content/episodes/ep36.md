## Overview

In 1946, Dennis Gabor published a paper titled "Theory of Communication," in which he proposed that any signal can be decomposed into elementary "acoustical quanta" — localized time-frequency atoms carrying the minimum possible uncertainty. This was not a metaphor borrowed from quantum mechanics; it was a precise mathematical claim: **a signal cannot simultaneously have arbitrarily sharp resolution in both time and frequency**. The constraint is written

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4\pi}{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}\sigma_t{{< /m >}} is the temporal spread and {{< m >}}\sigma_f{{< /m >}} is the spectral spread of any square-integrable signal of unit energy. This inequality — the Heisenberg-Gabor uncertainty principle — is not an approximation or an engineering limitation. It is a theorem derived from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in {{< m >}}L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}.

The episode builds from the proof of this inequality upward through three major structures. First, **Gabor atoms**: windowed complex sinusoids {{< m >}}g_{a,b}(t) = w(t-b)\,e^{2\pi i a t}{{< /m >}} that serve as elementary time-frequency cells. Projecting a signal onto a family of such atoms yields the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT). Second, **Gaussian optimality**: among all window shapes, only the Gaussian achieves the bound {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f = 1/(4\pi){{< /m >}} with equality, because the Fourier transform of a Gaussian is again a Gaussian. All other window shapes occupy a strictly larger area on the time-frequency plane. Third, **the Balian-Low theorem**: no Gabor system sampled at the critical density (one atom per unit area of the time-frequency plane) can form an orthonormal basis with a window that is simultaneously well-localized in both time and frequency. Perfect tiling of the plane and good localization are mathematically incompatible.

The musical consequences are direct. In granular synthesis, each grain is a physical realization of a Gabor atom — a windowed sinusoidal fragment placed at a specific time and pitch location. The uncertainty principle forces a fundamental tradeoff: short grains give precise rhythmic placement but smeared pitch; long grains give clear pitch but lose temporal precision. Xenakis exploited this tradeoff compositionally in the 1960s, and the same mathematics governs every modern audio codec's block-switching logic.

> *中文:* "1946年，Dennis Gabor在一篇论文里写道——声音由'声学量子'组成。他不是在借用物理学的隐喻，他在做一道数学题：任何信号，在时间上和频率上，不能同时无限精确。这就是时频不确定性，也是本集的主角。sigma t乘以sigma f，大于等于1除以4π。"

## Definitions

{{< definition name="Time-Frequency Spread" label="Definition 36.1" >}}
Let {{< m >}}x \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}\|x\|_2 = 1{{< /m >}} (unit energy). The **temporal mean** and **temporal variance** are

{{< dm >}}\mu_t = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t\,|x(t)|^2\,dt, \qquad \sigma_t^2 = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (t - \mu_t)^2\,|x(t)|^2\,dt{{< /dm >}}

The **spectral mean** and **spectral variance** are defined symmetrically using the Fourier transform {{< m >}}\hat{x}(f){{< /m >}}:

{{< dm >}}\mu_f = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f\,|\hat{x}(f)|^2\,df, \qquad \sigma_f^2 = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (f - \mu_f)^2\,|\hat{x}(f)|^2\,df{{< /dm >}}

Since {{< m >}}\|x\|_2 = 1{{< /m >}}, Parseval's theorem gives {{< m >}}\|\hat{x}\|_2 = 1{{< /m >}} as well, so {{< m >}}|x(t)|^2{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}|\hat{x}(f)|^2{{< /m >}} are valid probability density functions. The quantities {{< m >}}\sigma_t{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\sigma_f{{< /m >}} are the standard deviations of these distributions, measuring effective duration and effective bandwidth respectively.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Gabor Atom" label="Definition 36.2" >}}
A **Gabor atom** (also called a *logon* in Gabor's original 1946 paper) with time center {{< m >}}b \in \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}, frequency center {{< m >}}a \in \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}, and window function {{< m >}}w \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} is the function

{{< dm >}}g_{a,b}(t) = w(t - b)\cdot e^{2\pi i\, at}{{< /dm >}}

The parameter {{< m >}}b{{< /m >}} translates the window in time; the parameter {{< m >}}a{{< /m >}} modulates the carrier frequency. When {{< m >}}w{{< /m >}} is a normalized Gaussian, the atom {{< m >}}g_{a,b}{{< /m >}} achieves the minimum time-frequency area permitted by the uncertainty principle.

**Interpretation**: A Gabor atom is a complex sinusoid of frequency {{< m >}}a{{< /m >}} Hz "windowed" to a neighborhood of time {{< m >}}b{{< /m >}}. It occupies a rectangular cell of area proportional to {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f{{< /m >}} in the time-frequency plane.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)" label="Definition 36.3" >}}
Given a signal {{< m >}}x \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} and a window function {{< m >}}w \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}, the **Short-Time Fourier Transform** of {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}} is the function of two variables {{< m >}}(a, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2{{< /m >}} defined by

{{< dm >}}\text{STFT}\{x\}(a, b) = \langle x,\, g_{a,b}\rangle = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)\,\overline{w(t-b)}\,e^{-2\pi i\, at}\,dt{{< /dm >}}

The squared modulus {{< m >}}|\text{STFT}\{x\}(a,b)|^2{{< /m >}} is the **spectrogram** of {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}}: it gives the energy density of {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}} near time {{< m >}}b{{< /m >}} and frequency {{< m >}}a{{< /m >}}. The phase of {{< m >}}\text{STFT}\{x\}(a,b){{< /m >}} carries local frequency information used in the phase vocoder (EP35).
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Gabor Frame and Critical Sampling Density" label="Definition 36.4" >}}
Fix a window {{< m >}}w \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} and lattice parameters {{< m >}}\alpha, \beta > 0{{< /m >}}. The **Gabor system** generated by {{< m >}}(w, \alpha, \beta){{< /m >}} is the collection

{{< dm >}}\mathcal{G}(w, \alpha, \beta) = \bigl\{g_{m\alpha,\, n\beta}\bigr\}_{m, n \in \mathbb{Z}} = \bigl\{w(t - n\beta)\,e^{2\pi i\, m\alpha t}\bigr\}_{m,n \in \mathbb{Z}}{{< /dm >}}

The **sampling density** of this lattice is {{< m >}}\rho = 1/(\alpha\beta){{< /m >}}, measuring atoms per unit area of the time-frequency plane. The system {{< m >}}\mathcal{G}(w, \alpha, \beta){{< /m >}} is a **Gabor frame** if there exist constants {{< m >}}0 < A \leq B < \infty{{< /m >}} (frame bounds) such that for all {{< m >}}x \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}:

{{< dm >}}A\|x\|^2 \leq \sum_{m,n \in \mathbb{Z}} |\langle x, g_{m\alpha,\,n\beta}\rangle|^2 \leq B\|x\|^2{{< /dm >}}

The **critical density** is {{< m >}}\rho = 1{{< /m >}}, corresponding to {{< m >}}\alpha\beta = 1{{< /m >}} (one atom per unit time-frequency area). At densities {{< m >}}\rho > 1{{< /m >}} (oversampling), reconstruction is stable and non-unique; at {{< m >}}\rho < 1{{< /m >}} (undersampling), reconstruction fails.
{{< /definition >}}

## Main Theorems / 主要定理

{{< theorem name="Heisenberg-Gabor Uncertainty Principle" label="Theorem 36.1" >}}
Let {{< m >}}x \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} satisfy {{< m >}}\|x\|_2 = 1{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}tx(t) \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}, and {{< m >}}f\hat{x}(f) \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}. With {{< m >}}\sigma_t, \sigma_f{{< /m >}} as in Definition 36.1, we have

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \;\geq\; \frac{1}{4\pi}{{< /dm >}}

Equality holds if and only if {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}} is a (possibly time-shifted and frequency-modulated) Gaussian: {{< m >}}x(t) = C\,e^{-\alpha(t-b)^2+2\pi i a t}{{< /dm >}} for some {{< m >}}\alpha > 0{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}a, b, C \in \mathbb{C}{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}|C|{{< /m >}} chosen to give unit norm.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
Without loss of generality, assume {{< m >}}\mu_t = 0{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\mu_f = 0{{< /m >}} (the general case follows by modulating and shifting {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}}). We need to show

{{< dm >}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t^2 |x(t)|^2\,dt\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f^2 |\hat{x}(f)|^2\,df\right) \geq \frac{1}{16\pi^2}{{< /dm >}}

**Step 1: Relate frequency spread to a derivative.**

By the Fourier differentiation rule, {{< m >}}\widehat{x'}(f) = 2\pi i f \hat{x}(f){{< /m >}}, so

{{< dm >}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f^2 |\hat{x}(f)|^2\,df = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{|\widehat{x'}(f)|^2}{4\pi^2}\,df = \frac{\|x'\|_2^2}{4\pi^2}{{< /dm >}}

where the second equality uses Parseval's theorem. Therefore

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t^2 \cdot \sigma_f^2 = \left(\int t^2|x(t)|^2\,dt\right)\cdot \frac{\|x'\|_2^2}{4\pi^2}{{< /dm >}}

**Step 2: Apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.**

By Cauchy-Schwarz in {{< m >}}L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}},

{{< dm >}}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t\,x(t)\cdot \overline{x'(t)}\,dt\right|^2 \leq \left(\int t^2 |x(t)|^2\,dt\right)\left(\int |x'(t)|^2\,dt\right) = \sigma_t^2 \cdot \|x'\|_2^2{{< /dm >}}

**Step 3: Evaluate the left-hand side using integration by parts.**

{{< dm >}}\operatorname{Re}\!\left[\int t\,x(t)\,\overline{x'(t)}\,dt\right] = \frac{1}{2}\int t\,\frac{d}{dt}|x(t)|^2\,dt{{< /dm >}}

Integrating by parts (boundary term vanishes since {{< m >}}x \in L^2{{< /m >}} decays at infinity):

{{< dm >}}\frac{1}{2}\int t\,\frac{d}{dt}|x(t)|^2\,dt = -\frac{1}{2}\int |x(t)|^2\,dt = -\frac{1}{2}{{< /dm >}}

using {{< m >}}\|x\|_2 = 1{{< /m >}}. Therefore the left-hand side of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality satisfies

{{< dm >}}\left|\int t\,x(t)\,\overline{x'(t)}\,dt\right| \geq \left|\operatorname{Re}\!\left[\int t\,x(t)\,\overline{x'(t)}\,dt\right]\right| = \frac{1}{2}{{< /dm >}}

**Step 4: Combine.**

From Steps 2 and 3:

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t^2 \cdot \|x'\|_2^2 \geq \frac{1}{4}{{< /dm >}}

Multiplying both sides by {{< m >}}1/(4\pi^2){{< /m >}} and using Step 1:

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t^2 \cdot \sigma_f^2 = \sigma_t^2 \cdot \frac{\|x'\|_2^2}{4\pi^2} \geq \frac{1}{16\pi^2}{{< /dm >}}

Taking square roots: {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \geq \dfrac{1}{4\pi}{{< /m >}}.

**Equality condition**: Cauchy-Schwarz is an equality if and only if {{< m >}}x'(t) = \lambda t\,x(t){{< /m >}} for some constant {{< m >}}\lambda{{< /m >}}. The reality condition (from the integration-by-parts step) forces {{< m >}}\lambda{{< /m >}} to be real and negative, say {{< m >}}\lambda = -2\alpha{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}\alpha > 0{{< /m >}}. The ODE {{< m >}}x'(t) = -2\alpha t\,x(t){{< /m >}} has general solution {{< m >}}x(t) = C\,e^{-\alpha t^2}{{< /m >}}, a Gaussian. Including the displaced/modulated general case gives the stated equality condition. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

> *中文:* "把这个不等式写清楚。设一个信号以t为中心，时间宽度是sigma t，频率集中在某频带，宽度sigma f。Cauchy-Schwarz不等式告诉我们，两个L2函数内积的平方，小于等于各自模的平方之积。把这个应用到信号与其导数，就得到sigma t乘以sigma f大于等于1除以4π。"

{{< theorem name="Gaussian Optimality: Unique Minimizer of Time-Frequency Area" label="Theorem 36.2" >}}
Among all unit-norm functions {{< m >}}x \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}, the **only** functions achieving the equality

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f = \frac{1}{4\pi}{{< /dm >}}

are Gaussian atoms of the form {{< m >}}x(t) = C\,e^{-\alpha(t-b)^2 + 2\pi i a t}{{< /m >}} ({{< m >}}\alpha > 0{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}a, b \in \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}C{{< /m >}} normalization constant). In particular, the standard Gaussian

{{< dm >}}g(t) = \left(\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{1/4} e^{-\alpha t^2}{{< /dm >}}

has Fourier transform

{{< dm >}}\hat{g}(f) = \left(\frac{2}{\alpha\pi}\right)^{1/4} e^{-\pi^2 f^2/\alpha}{{< /dm >}}

which is again a Gaussian. No other window shape (rectangular, Hann, Hamming, Blackman, etc.) achieves {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f = 1/(4\pi){{< /m >}}.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
**Existence (Gaussian achieves equality)**:

For {{< m >}}g(t) = C\,e^{-\alpha t^2}{{< /m >}} (with {{< m >}}\mu_t = 0{{< /m >}} by symmetry), compute directly:

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t^2 = \int t^2 \cdot C^2 e^{-2\alpha t^2}\,dt \Big/ \int C^2 e^{-2\alpha t^2}\,dt = \frac{\int t^2 e^{-2\alpha t^2}\,dt}{\int e^{-2\alpha t^2}\,dt}{{< /dm >}}

Using Gaussian integrals {{< m >}}\int e^{-2\alpha t^2}dt = \sqrt{\pi/(2\alpha)}{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\int t^2 e^{-2\alpha t^2}dt = \sqrt{\pi}/(4\alpha)^{3/2} \cdot \alpha = \sqrt{\pi}/(4\sqrt{2\alpha}\cdot 2\alpha){{< /m >}}, we get {{< m >}}\sigma_t^2 = 1/(4\alpha){{< /m >}}.

The Fourier transform is {{< m >}}\hat{g}(f) \propto e^{-\pi^2 f^2/\alpha}{{< /m >}}, another Gaussian with parameter {{< m >}}\alpha' = \pi^2/\alpha{{< /m >}} in the exponent. By the same calculation, {{< m >}}\sigma_f^2 = \alpha/(4\pi^2){{< /m >}}.

Therefore:

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\alpha}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{4\pi}{{< /dm >}}

confirming that the Gaussian achieves equality.

**Uniqueness (only Gaussian achieves equality)**:

This follows directly from the equality condition in Theorem 36.1: the Cauchy-Schwarz step is an equality if and only if the two functions in the inner product are proportional. As shown there, this forces {{< m >}}x'(t) = -2\alpha t\,x(t){{< /m >}} for some {{< m >}}\alpha > 0{{< /m >}}, whose only {{< m >}}L^2{{< /m >}} solutions are Gaussians. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}

**Comparison with other windows**: For a rectangular window of width {{< m >}}T{{< /m >}}, one can compute {{< m >}}\sigma_t = T/(2\sqrt{3}){{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\sigma_f \approx \infty{{< /m >}} (since the sinc-function spectrum has infinite variance). For a Hann window, the spectral variance is finite but {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \approx 0.083 > 1/(4\pi) \approx 0.080{{< /m >}}, strictly exceeding the Gaussian bound.
{{< /proof >}}

> *中文:* "高斯函数g of t等于e的负t平方次方，是唯一能达到不等式等号的函数：sigma t乘以sigma f恰好等于1除以4π。原因在于，高斯函数的傅里叶变换还是高斯函数——时域紧凑，频域同样紧凑。没有其他形状能做到这一点。"

{{< theorem name="Balian-Low Theorem" label="Theorem 36.3" >}}
Let {{< m >}}w \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\alpha, \beta > 0{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}\alpha\beta = 1{{< /m >}} (critical sampling density). If the Gabor system {{< m >}}\mathcal{G}(w, \alpha, \beta){{< /m >}} forms an orthonormal basis (or more generally, a Riesz basis) for {{< m >}}L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}}, then

{{< dm >}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |tw(t)|^2\,dt = +\infty \quad \text{or} \quad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |f\hat{w}(f)|^2\,df = +\infty{{< /dm >}}

That is, either the window has infinite temporal variance, or it has infinite spectral variance (or both). It is impossible to have a critically sampled Gabor Riesz basis whose window is simultaneously well-localized in both time and frequency.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
We sketch the proof via the **Zak transform**, which is the standard tool for studying critical-density Gabor systems.

**Step 1: The Zak transform.**

For {{< m >}}\alpha\beta = 1{{< /m >}} (say {{< m >}}\alpha = \beta = 1{{< /m >}} without loss of generality), the **Zak transform** of {{< m >}}w \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} is

{{< dm >}}(Zw)(t, f) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} w(t - n)\,e^{2\pi i n f}, \qquad (t,f) \in [0,1)^2{{< /dm >}}

The Zak transform is a unitary map from {{< m >}}L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} to {{< m >}}L^2([0,1)^2){{< /m >}}. The Gabor system {{< m >}}\mathcal{G}(w, 1, 1){{< /m >}} is an orthonormal basis for {{< m >}}L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} if and only if {{< m >}}|(Zw)(t,f)| = 1{{< /m >}} for almost every {{< m >}}(t,f) \in [0,1)^2{{< /m >}}.

**Step 2: Continuity forces a zero.**

A fundamental property of the Zak transform is that if {{< m >}}w{{< /m >}} is continuous and decays sufficiently rapidly, then {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}} is continuous on {{< m >}}[0,1)^2{{< /m >}} and satisfies the **quasi-periodicity** condition:

{{< dm >}}(Zw)(t+1, f) = e^{2\pi i f}(Zw)(t,f), \qquad (Zw)(t, f+1) = (Zw)(t,f){{< /dm >}}

By the quasi-periodicity, tracing {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}} around the boundary of {{< m >}}[0,1)^2{{< /m >}} as a closed loop in {{< m >}}\mathbb{C}{{< /m >}}, the winding number of the boundary must equal 1. By the argument principle, a continuous function with winding number 1 around a domain boundary must have **at least one zero** inside the domain. Therefore {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}} cannot have {{< m >}}|Zw| = 1{{< /m >}} everywhere — it must vanish somewhere.

**Step 3: Zero of Zak transform ↔ unbounded localization.**

The Zak transform has an expansion in terms of the Fourier series coefficients of {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}}; the decay of those coefficients is controlled by the localization of {{< m >}}w{{< /m >}}. A precise calculation (using Poisson summation) shows:

If both {{< m >}}\int t^2 |w(t)|^2\,dt < \infty{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\int f^2 |\hat{w}(f)|^2\,df < \infty{{< /m >}}, then {{< m >}}w{{< /m >}} belongs to the Sobolev space {{< m >}}W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}} is continuous. Combined with Step 2, the Zak transform then has a zero, preventing {{< m >}}|Zw| = 1{{< /m >}} everywhere. This contradicts the orthonormality condition from Step 1.

**Conclusion:** For {{< m >}}\mathcal{G}(w, 1, 1){{< /m >}} to be an orthonormal basis, {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}} must equal 1 in modulus a.e. But good localization forces {{< m >}}Zw{{< /m >}} to be continuous and hence to have a zero. The two requirements are incompatible: one of the two integrability conditions on {{< m >}}t^2|w|^2{{< /m >}} or {{< m >}}f^2|\hat{w}|^2{{< /m >}} must fail, i.e., at least one of {{< m >}}\sigma_t, \sigma_f{{< /m >}} is infinite. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

> *中文:* "Balian-Low定理的回答是：不能。如果Gabor框架在临界密度下采样，也就是每个时频格恰好有一个原子，那么窗函数要么时域无界，要么频域无界。你不能同时拥有完美铺砌和良好局域化。"

{{< proposition label="Prop 36.4" name="Oversampling as the Practical Resolution" >}}
Let {{< m >}}\alpha\beta < 1{{< /m >}} (oversampling; density {{< m >}}\rho = 1/(\alpha\beta) > 1{{< /m >}}). Then the Balian-Low obstruction does not apply: it is possible to construct a Gabor frame {{< m >}}\mathcal{G}(w, \alpha, \beta){{< /m >}} with a window {{< m >}}w{{< /m >}} that is well-localized in both time and frequency (finite {{< m >}}\sigma_t{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\sigma_f{{< /m >}}), while still enabling stable reconstruction of any {{< m >}}x \in L^2(\mathbb{R}){{< /m >}} via the frame expansion

{{< dm >}}x = \sum_{m,n} \langle x, g_{m\alpha, n\beta}\rangle\,\tilde{g}_{m\alpha, n\beta}{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}\{\tilde{g}_{m\alpha, n\beta}\}{{< /m >}} is the **dual frame**. In particular, a Gaussian window with any hop size {{< m >}}\beta < 1/\alpha{{< /m >}} yields a valid frame.
{{< /proposition >}}

{{< proof >}}
The Balian-Low theorem is stated only for the critical case {{< m >}}\alpha\beta = 1{{< /m >}}. The proof uses the Zak transform winding-number argument, which applies specifically when the Gabor system is required to be a Riesz basis (one atom per unit area). At overcritical density {{< m >}}\alpha\beta < 1{{< /m >}}, the system is **overcomplete**: there are more atoms than needed for a basis, so the winding-number constraint is no longer binding. A Gaussian {{< m >}}w(t) = e^{-\pi t^2}{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}\alpha\beta < 1{{< /m >}} generates a Gabor frame with both frame bounds finite and positive (this follows from the theory of Weierstrass sigma functions; see Lyubarskii 1992 and Seip & Wallstén 1992). The dual frame atoms {{< m >}}\tilde{g}_{m\alpha, n\beta}{{< /m >}} are computable from the frame operator and also have finite localization. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

| 100 ms | 25 ms | {{< m >}}\geq 3.2{{< /m >}} Hz | Narrow | Pitch clear; about 5 cents at A4 |
| 500 ms | 125 ms | {{< m >}}\geq 0.64{{< /m >}} Hz | Very narrow | Pitch very clear; temporal envelope lost |

**Derivation for T = 20 ms**:

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t = 5 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s}, \qquad \sigma_f \geq \frac{1}{4\pi \sigma_t} = \frac{1}{4\pi \times 0.005} = \frac{1}{0.0628} \approx 15.9\ \text{Hz}{{< /dm >}}

At A4 = 440 Hz, a bandwidth of ±15.9 Hz corresponds to a frequency uncertainty of ±15.9/440 ≈ ±3.6%, roughly ±62 cents — close to a semitone. In granular synthesis this manifests as perceptibly rough pitch when grains are 20 ms long.

> *中文:* "颗粒越短，时间分辨率越高——你能精确控制声音在哪一刻出现。但颗粒太短，sigma f变大，音高变得模糊，听起来是'咔哒'声，不是音符。颗粒越长，音高越清晰，但时间感消失，声音变成绵延的织体。"

**Critical density example**:

For STFT with hop size {{< m >}}H{{< /m >}} and window length {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} samples at sample rate {{< m >}}f_s{{< /m >}}:

- Time resolution: {{< m >}}\Delta t = H/f_s{{< /m >}}
- Frequency resolution: {{< m >}}\Delta f = f_s/N{{< /m >}}
- Sampling density: {{< m >}}\rho = 1/(\Delta t \cdot \Delta f) = N/H{{< /m >}}

For the CD standard with 44100 Hz, {{< m >}}N = 2048{{< /m >}} samples and {{< m >}}H = 512{{< /m >}} samples (75% overlap): {{< m >}}\rho = 2048/512 = 4{{< /m >}}. This is four times the critical density, well within the regime where the Balian-Low obstruction is absent and a well-localized Gaussian window can be used.

## Musical Connection / 音乐联系

{{< musical-connection >}}
**From information theory to acoustical quanta**

{{< episode-ref ep="7" >}}EP07 (Shannon information theory){{< /episode-ref >}} established that the information capacity of a channel is bounded by bandwidth and signal-to-noise ratio. Gabor's 1946 paper drew an explicit parallel: if information in a signal is localized both in time and frequency, then the *number of distinguishable information cells* per unit time-frequency area is bounded. A Gabor atom occupies the minimum possible area on the time-frequency plane, so it is the most efficient carrier of one "quantum" of acoustic information. The fundamental unit is the **logon**, carrying approximately {{< m >}}\log_2(1 + \text{SNR}){{< /m >}} bits — Shannon's formula written for a single time-frequency cell. This is the same Shannon entropy from EP07, now geometrically embodied in a single atom of sound.

**Granular synthesis: Xenakis and Curtis Roads**

{{< episode-ref ep="26" >}}EP26 (1/f statistics and granular synthesis){{< /episode-ref >}} introduced Curtis Roads' discovery that grain densities in natural granular textures exhibit long-range power-law correlations — the grains are not scattered randomly but cluster on multiple time scales. The uncertainty principle explains *why* different grain lengths are auditorily distinct: a 5 ms grain is mathematically incapable of carrying a definite pitch; a 200 ms grain cannot convey a sharp rhythmic attack. Xenakis composed with clouds of short grains in *Analogique A/B* (1958–59) and *Granular Synthesis* (1970) precisely because he wanted sound textures that occupied specific regions of the time-frequency plane without crystallizing into definite pitch or rhythm — a compositional exploitation of the uncertainty principle. Roads' fractal grain statistics from EP26 are a further layer: not only is each individual grain constrained by the inequality, but the distribution of grains across time scales is itself scale-invariant.

**MDCT block switching: uncertainty as adaptive design**

The forward reference to {{< episode-ref ep="40" >}}EP40 (MDCT and perceptual audio coding){{< /episode-ref >}} is a direct application. Modern audio codecs (MP3, AAC, Vorbis) use MDCT blocks of two sizes: a "long" block (2048 samples at 44100 Hz ≈ 46 ms) for stationary signals, and "short" blocks (256 samples ≈ 6 ms) for transients. This is an engineering implementation of the uncertainty tradeoff: a long block gives good frequency resolution (narrow {{< m >}}\sigma_f{{< /m >}}) for stable tones, but poor time localization (wide {{< m >}}\sigma_t{{< /m >}}). If a percussive transient arrives mid-block, the coding artifacts spread over the entire block duration — an audible "pre-echo." Switching to short blocks reduces {{< m >}}\sigma_t{{< /m >}} at the cost of increased {{< m >}}\sigma_f{{< /m >}}, sacrificing frequency resolution for temporal accuracy. The block-switching decision algorithm (detecting signal stationarity via sub-band energy variance) is an automated traversal of the {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \geq 1/(4\pi){{< /m >}} constraint surface.

**The STFT and COLA: Balian-Low in engineering form**

{{< episode-ref ep="35" >}}EP35 (Phase Vocoder and STFT){{< /episode-ref >}} introduced the Constant Overlap-Add (COLA) condition as the requirement for perfect reconstruction. COLA requires that the sum of time-shifted window copies equals a constant, which is precisely the synthesis frame condition for an oversampled Gabor system. The Balian-Low theorem explains *why* engineers do not use critically sampled STFT: at {{< m >}}\alpha\beta = 1{{< /m >}}, no well-localized window can yield a reconstruction basis. The ubiquitous 75% overlap ({{< m >}}\rho = 4{{< /m >}}) in production audio tools is not merely an empirical preference — it reflects the theoretical necessity established by Balian and Low.

**The rectangle-sinc duality (EP30)**

The rectangular window used in naive STFT analysis is an extreme case of the uncertainty tradeoff. The Fourier transform of a rectangular window of width {{< m >}}T{{< /m >}} is {{< m >}}\text{sinc}(fT){{< /m >}}, which decays as {{< m >}}1/f{{< /m >}} and has infinite spectral variance ({{< m >}}\sigma_f = \infty{{< /m >}}). Conversely, a sinc window in the time domain — which has perfect spectral concentration — is infinitely long in time ({{< m >}}\sigma_t = \infty{{< /m >}}). These two extremes — sharp time boundary with infinite frequency spread, and sharp frequency boundary with infinite time spread — are the two poles of the uncertainty principle, separated by the entire space of intermediate windows with the Gaussian sitting at the minimum-area point.

## Limits and Open Questions / 局限性与开放问题

1. **Infinite support of the Gaussian window**: The Gaussian {{< m >}}e^{-\alpha t^2}{{< /m >}} is the unique minimizer of {{< m >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f{{< /m >}}, but it has infinite support and cannot be implemented exactly in any digital system. Real implementations use truncated Gaussians (multiplied by a compact-support window), which break the equality but can be made arbitrarily close to it. Quantifying the tradeoff between truncation length and deviation from the uncertainty bound is a practical optimization problem without a universally agreed solution.

2. **Balian-Low for frames (not just Riesz bases)**: The classical Balian-Low theorem applies to Riesz bases. For general Gabor frames at critical density (not necessarily Riesz), the situation is more delicate. The result extends in various ways, but the precise characterization of which window functions are admissible remains an active research area in time-frequency analysis.

3. **Multiwindow and vector-valued Gabor systems**: Using multiple windows simultaneously (superframes) can partially circumvent the Balian-Low obstruction. A system of {{< m >}}K{{< /m >}} windows at density {{< m >}}\rho = 1/K{{< /m >}} each (total density 1) can have all windows well-localized. This is related to the theory of MIMO communication and has potential applications in multi-channel audio analysis, but the optimal construction of such multiwindow systems is not yet fully characterized.

4. **Heisenberg uncertainty vs. Rényi entropy uncertainty**: The classical uncertainty principle measures spread via variance ({{< m >}}\sigma_t, \sigma_f{{< /m >}}). Alternative formulations use Rényi entropy or Wehrl entropy instead of second moments, yielding tighter or different uncertainty inequalities. Whether these entropic uncertainty principles have a cleaner musical or perceptual interpretation than the Heisenberg formulation is an open question.

5. **Discrete Gabor analysis**: The continuous theory developed here requires adaptation for finite discrete signals. For a length-{{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} DFT, the Balian-Low theorem does not apply in its classical form, and perfect critically sampled Gabor bases do exist (e.g., the Zak-domain construction). However, for large {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}}, the discrete and continuous theories converge, and the same localization tradeoffs become apparent.

{{< conjecture name="Optimal Block-Switching Threshold" >}}
In a two-block-size MDCT codec (long block length {{< m >}}N_L{{< /m >}}, short block length {{< m >}}N_S{{< /m >}}), the optimal switching threshold — the signal non-stationarity measure above which switching to short blocks minimizes total perceptual distortion — should be expressible as a function of the ratio {{< m >}}N_S/N_L{{< /m >}} and the masked threshold shape from the auditory model alone, independently of bit rate and sample rate.

**Falsifiability criterion**: If experiments with a standardized auditory model (e.g., ISO 532B loudness) and variable bit rates show that the optimal threshold varies with bit rate at fixed {{< m >}}N_S/N_L{{< /m >}}, the conjecture is falsified — implying that the coding rate itself must enter the block-switching decision.
