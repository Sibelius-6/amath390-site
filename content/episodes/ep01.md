## Overview

If I told you that moving from a C major chord to an A minor chord takes exactly one step in a certain geometric space — and that this space has the shape of a donut — would you believe it?

This episode develops the mathematical backbone of **Neo-Riemannian theory**: three involutory chord transformations (P, L, R) that generate a dihedral group acting on 24 major/minor triads, a torus-shaped visualization called the **Tonnetz**, and Dmitri Tymoczko's general theory of **voice-leading orbifolds**.

> *中文:* "从数学角度看，这三种变换生成了一个群。具体来说，是二面体群D12，一共有24个元素。这个群作用在24个大小三和弦上，而且是simply transitive的。"

## Definitions

{{< definition name="Major and Minor Triad" label="Definition 1.1" >}}
A **triad** is an ordered triple of pitch classes {{< m >}}(r, m, \ell) \in \mathbb{Z}_{12}^3{{< /m >}} satisfying the spacing constraint:

- **Major triad** rooted at {{< m >}}r{{< /m >}}: pitch classes {{< m >}}\{r,\; r+4,\; r+7\} \pmod{12}{{< /m >}}
- **Minor triad** rooted at {{< m >}}r{{< /m >}}: pitch classes {{< m >}}\{r,\; r+3,\; r+7\} \pmod{12}{{< /m >}}

There are 12 major triads and 12 minor triads, giving a set {{< m >}}\mathcal{T}{{< /m >}} of 24 triads. We write {{< m >}}[r,+]{{< /m >}} for the major triad rooted at {{< m >}}r{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}[r,-]{{< /m >}} for the minor triad rooted at {{< m >}}r{{< /m >}}.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="PLR Transformations" label="Definition 1.2" >}}
Three functions {{< m >}}P, L, R : \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{T}{{< /m >}} are defined by the rule: each transformation preserves two common tones and moves the remaining tone by the minimal semitone distance.

- **P (Parallel)**: changes mode while keeping root fixed.
  {{< dm >}}P([r,+]) = [r,-], \qquad P([r,-]) = [r,+]{{< /dm >}}
  Concretely: in {{< m >}}[r,+] = \{r, r{+}4, r{+}7\}{{< /m >}}, the note {{< m >}}r{+}4{{< /m >}} moves by one semitone to {{< m >}}r{+}3{{< /m >}}.

- **L (Leading-tone exchange)**: in a major triad, the root moves down by semitone; in minor, the fifth moves up by semitone.
  {{< dm >}}L([r,+]) = [r+4,-], \qquad L([r,-]) = [r+8,+]{{< /dm >}}
  (indices mod 12)

- **R (Relative)**: relates a major triad to its relative minor (sharing the same key signature).
  {{< dm >}}R([r,+]) = [r+9,-], \qquad R([r,-]) = [r+3,+]{{< /dm >}}

Each of P, L, R is an **involution**: {{< m >}}P^2 = L^2 = R^2 = \mathrm{id}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /definition >}}

> *Visual:* C major {{< m >}}\{C, E, G\}{{< /m >}} under P becomes C minor {{< m >}}\{C, E\flat, G\}{{< /m >}} — only E moves, dropping one semitone to E{{< m >}}\flat{{< /m >}}. Under R it becomes A minor {{< m >}}\{A, C, E\}{{< /m >}} — only G moves, ascending a whole tone to A.

{{< definition name="Neo-Riemannian Group" label="Definition 1.3" >}}
The **Neo-Riemannian group** {{< m >}}\mathcal{R}{{< /m >}} is the subgroup of {{< m >}}\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{T}){{< /m >}} generated by {{< m >}}\{P, L, R\}{{< /m >}}:

{{< dm >}}\mathcal{R} = \langle P, L, R \rangle \leq \mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{T}){{< /dm >}}

Since {{< m >}}P = (LR)^3 \cdot L{{< /m >}} (a provable relation), {{< m >}}\mathcal{R}{{< /m >}} is already generated by {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}} alone. As a group, {{< m >}}\mathcal{R} \cong D_{24}{{< /m >}}, the dihedral group of order 24.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Tonnetz" label="Definition 1.4" >}}
The **Tonnetz** (German: "tone network") is a graph {{< m >}}G = (V, E){{< /m >}} where:

- **Vertices** {{< m >}}V = \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}: the 12 pitch classes.
- **Edges** {{< m >}}E{{< /m >}}: pairs {{< m >}}\{a, b\}{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}|a - b| \equiv 7 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} (perfect fifth), {{< m >}}|a - b| \equiv 4 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} (major third), or {{< m >}}|a - b| \equiv 3 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} (minor third).

Each triangular face of this planar graph corresponds to a triad: upward-pointing triangles are major triads, downward-pointing triangles are minor triads.

A PLR transformation corresponds to a **flip across a shared edge**: P flips across the minor-third edge, L flips across the major-third edge, R flips across the perfect-fifth edge.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Voice-Leading Distance" label="Definition 1.5" >}}
The **voice-leading distance** between two chords {{< m >}}A = \{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}B = \{b_1, \ldots, b_n\}{{< /m >}} (as multisets in {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}) is

{{< dm >}}d_{\mathrm{VL}}(A, B) = \min_{\sigma \in S_n} \sum_{i=1}^n \min_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} |a_i - b_{\sigma(i)} + 12k|{{< /dm >}}

where the outer minimum is over all bijections {{< m >}}\sigma{{< /m >}} pairing voices, and the inner minimum realizes the shortest semitone displacement respecting octave equivalence.
{{< /definition >}}

## Main Theorems

{{< theorem name="PLR Are Involutions" label="Theorem 1.1" >}}
Each of the three transformations {{< m >}}P{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}} is an involution on {{< m >}}\mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}:
{{< dm >}}P^2 = L^2 = R^2 = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{T}}{{< /dm >}}
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
We verify for {{< m >}}P{{< /m >}}; the argument for {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}} is analogous.

By Definition 1.2, {{< m >}}P([r,+]) = [r,-]{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}P([r,-]) = [r,+]{{< /m >}}.
Thus {{< m >}}P^2([r,+]) = P(P([r,+])) = P([r,-]) = [r,+]{{< /m >}}, and similarly {{< m >}}P^2([r,-]) = [r,-]{{< /m >}}.
Since {{< m >}}P^2{{< /m >}} fixes every element of {{< m >}}\mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}, we have {{< m >}}P^2 = \mathrm{id}{{< /m >}}. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="Simple Transitivity of D₁₂ on Triads" label="Theorem 1.2" >}}
The Neo-Riemannian group {{< m >}}\mathcal{R} \cong D_{24}{{< /m >}} acts **simply transitively** on {{< m >}}\mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}: for any two triads {{< m >}}s, t \in \mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}, there exists a **unique** group element {{< m >}}g \in \mathcal{R}{{< /m >}} such that {{< m >}}g \cdot s = t{{< /m >}}.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
**Transitivity** (any triad reachable from any other): The 24 triads form a single orbit under {{< m >}}\mathcal{R}{{< /m >}}. Starting from {{< m >}}[C,+]{{< /m >}}, repeated application of {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}} generates a connected path through all 24 triads — this can be verified by explicitly tracing the "Chicken-wire" cycle {{< m >}}L, R, L, R, \ldots{{< /m >}} (the 24-cycle that returns to the start), which visits all elements of {{< m >}}\mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}.

**Free action** (no nonidentity element fixes any triad): {{< m >}}|\mathcal{R}| = 24 = |\mathcal{T}|{{< /m >}}. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem,
{{< dm >}}|\mathcal{R}| = |\mathrm{Orb}(s)| \cdot |\mathrm{Stab}(s)|{{< /dm >}}
Since {{< m >}}|\mathrm{Orb}(s)| = 24{{< /m >}} (transitivity) and {{< m >}}|\mathcal{R}| = 24{{< /m >}}, we get {{< m >}}|\mathrm{Stab}(s)| = 1{{< /m >}}, meaning only the identity fixes any element. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

> *Visual:* Place all 24 triads as vertices of a 24-gon. Draw edges for each generator: blue edges for {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}}, red edges for {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}}, green edges for {{< m >}}P{{< /m >}}. The result is a Cayley graph of {{< m >}}D_{24}{{< /m >}} — every vertex has exactly three colored edges emanating from it.

{{< theorem name="Tonnetz Torus Identification" label="Theorem 1.3" >}}
Under enharmonic equivalence (identifying {{< m >}}G\sharp = A\flat{{< /m >}}, etc.), the Tonnetz wraps into a torus {{< m >}}T^2 = S^1 \times S^1{{< /m >}}.

Specifically:
1. Ascending by a perfect fifth (7 semitones) twelve times returns to the starting pitch class: {{< m >}}0 \xrightarrow{+7} 7 \xrightarrow{+7} 2 \xrightarrow{+7} \cdots \xrightarrow{+7} 0 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}}. This identifies opposite vertical edges.
2. Ascending by a major third (4 semitones) three times traverses the augmented triad and returns: {{< m >}}0 \xrightarrow{+4} 4 \xrightarrow{+4} 8 \xrightarrow{+4} 0 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}}. Combined with the {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} structure, this identifies opposite horizontal edges.

The resulting quotient space is homeomorphic to the torus {{< m >}}T^2{{< /m >}}.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
The Tonnetz can be given coordinates in {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12} \times \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} (or more precisely, in {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12} \times \mathbb{Z}_3{{< /m >}} with the diagonal fifth-direction). The key point is that the lattice of pitch classes generated by the intervals {{< m >}}\{3, 4, 7\}{{< /m >}} modulo 12 is a quotient {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}^2 / \Lambda{{< /m >}} for some sublattice {{< m >}}\Lambda{{< /m >}}. The identifications imposed by {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} (the group of pitch classes) cause both pairs of opposite sides of the fundamental domain to be glued with the same orientation, producing {{< m >}}T^2{{< /m >}} rather than {{< m >}}S^2{{< /m >}} or {{< m >}}K{{< /m >}} (the Klein bottle). The formal identification is: the fifth-direction generator {{< m >}}e_1{{< /m >}} satisfies {{< m >}}12 e_1 = 0{{< /m >}}, and the third-direction generator {{< m >}}e_2{{< /m >}} satisfies {{< m >}}3 e_2 = 0{{< /m >}} within the fundamental domain, with both identifications orientation-preserving. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="Tymoczko Orbifold" label="Theorem 1.4" >}}
The space of {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-voice chords (unordered multisets of {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}} pitch classes in {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}) is the orbifold

{{< dm >}}\mathcal{O}_n = T^n / S_n{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}T^n = (\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z})^n{{< /m >}} is the {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-torus of ordered {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-tuples, and {{< m >}}S_n{{< /m >}} acts by permuting coordinates.

Special cases:
- {{< m >}}n = 2{{< /m >}}: {{< m >}}T^2 / S_2 \cong{{< /m >}} Möbius band (with boundary = unison/octave dyads)
- {{< m >}}n = 3{{< /m >}}: {{< m >}}T^3 / S_3 \cong{{< /m >}} a twisted triangular prism with three singular edges

Voice-leading distance {{< m >}}d_{\mathrm{VL}}{{< /m >}} equals the path length in {{< m >}}\mathcal{O}_n{{< /m >}} under the quotient metric.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
(Sketch) {{< m >}}T^n{{< /m >}} carries the flat metric from {{< m >}}(\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z})^n{{< /m >}}. The action of {{< m >}}S_n{{< /m >}} by coordinate permutation is an isometric group action, so the quotient {{< m >}}T^n/S_n{{< /m >}} inherits a well-defined metric (on the regular part) and becomes an orbifold at fixed points of the {{< m >}}S_n{{< /m >}}-action (i.e., at chords with repeated notes). The geodesic distance in {{< m >}}\mathcal{O}_n{{< /m >}} between two points {{< m >}}[A]{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}[B]{{< /m >}} is

{{< dm >}}d_{\mathcal{O}_n}([A],[B]) = \min_{\sigma \in S_n} d_{T^n}(A, \sigma(B)){{< /dm >}}

which is exactly the formula for {{< m >}}d_{\mathrm{VL}}{{< /m >}} in Definition 1.5. The homeomorphism type of {{< m >}}\mathcal{O}_2{{< /m >}} follows from the standard identification of the quotient of {{< m >}}[0,12)^2{{< /m >}} by {{< m >}}(a,b) \sim (b,a){{< /m >}} as a Möbius band when one accounts for the periodic boundary. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< proposition label="Prop 1.1" name="Parsimony of PLR" >}}
Each PLR transformation moves exactly one pitch class by one or two semitones and fixes the other two. More precisely:
- {{< m >}}P{{< /m >}} moves one note by exactly 1 semitone (the third of the triad).
- {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}} moves one note by exactly 1 semitone (the root of a major triad, or the fifth of a minor triad).
- {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}} moves one note by exactly 2 semitones (the fifth of a major triad, or the root of a minor triad).

Consequently, {{< m >}}d_{\mathrm{VL}}(t, P(t)) \leq 1{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}d_{\mathrm{VL}}(t, L(t)) \leq 1{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}d_{\mathrm{VL}}(t, R(t)) \leq 2{{< /m >}} for all {{< m >}}t \in \mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /proposition >}}

{{< proof >}}
Direct computation. For {{< m >}}P{{< /m >}}: in {{< m >}}[C,+] = \{0,4,7\}{{< /m >}}, we have {{< m >}}P([C,+]) = [C,-] = \{0,3,7\}{{< /m >}}. The note 4 (E) moves to 3 (E{{< m >}}\flat{{< /m >}}), a displacement of 1 semitone; notes 0 (C) and 7 (G) are fixed. For {{< m >}}L{{< /m >}}: {{< m >}}L([C,+]) = [E,-] = \{4,7,11\}{{< /m >}}. Note 0 (C) moves to 11 (B), a displacement of 1 semitone downward; notes 4 and 7 are fixed. For {{< m >}}R{{< /m >}}: {{< m >}}R([C,+]) = [A,-] = \{9,0,4\}{{< /m >}}. Note 7 (G) moves to 9 (A), a displacement of 2 semitones; notes 0 and 4 are fixed. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

## Musical Connection

{{< musical-connection >}}
**The Tristan Chord and the Tonnetz**

Wagner's opera *Tristan und Isolde* (1865) opens with the famous "Tristan chord" {{< m >}}\{F, B, D\sharp, G\sharp\}{{< /m >}}, which resisted definitive functional-harmonic analysis for over a century. Neo-Riemannian theory offers an alternative reading.

The chord progression in the opening bars — Tristan chord → E major → (continuation) — can be tracked as a path on the Tonnetz. The Tristan chord is an instance of a "French augmented sixth" spelling, but on the Tonnetz it occupies a position adjacent to multiple triads. The initial resolution traces a short geodesic on {{< m >}}T^2{{< /m >}}.

> *中文:* "在Tonnetz上，我们可以清晰地追踪它的路径。这些和弦在环面上形成了一个优美的轨迹，绕着Torus表面滑行。"

**Romantic-era chromatic harmony** exploited exactly the smooth voice-leading that the Tonnetz geometry quantifies. Schubert's "Wanderer" Fantasy and Liszt's harmonic progressions follow paths through the Tonnetz that minimize total voice-leading displacement — instinctively approaching the geodesics that Tymoczko's orbifold formalism later made rigorous.

**Practical use in analysis**: music theorist Brian Hyer (1995) and Richard Cohn (1996–1998) revived 19th-century Riemannian ideas precisely because PLR transformations capture how chromatic composers modulated through unrelated keys with minimal voice-leading effort. The mathematical structure predicts which progressions will sound "smooth" even when harmonically distant in the traditional functional sense.
{{< /musical-connection >}}

## Limits and Open Questions

1. **Extension to seventh chords**: PLR theory is cleanest for triads. Extending to dominant seventh chords, half-diminished sevenths, etc. requires defining additional generators and working in a larger group. The resulting orbifold {{< m >}}T^4/S_4{{< /m >}} is considerably more complex.

2. **Metric vs. combinatorial voice-leading**: Definition 1.5 gives a metric, but actual voice-leading in score involves register (which octave each note is in) and voice-crossing constraints. A full model requires lifting from {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} to {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} and imposing ordering constraints.

3. **Perception vs. geometry**: It remains an open empirical question whether listeners perceive voice-leading distance as the geodesic metric in {{< m >}}\mathcal{O}_n{{< /m >}}. Psychoacoustic experiments (e.g., Callender, Quinn, Tymoczko 2008) support the orbifold model, but individual variation and musical context complicate direct verification.

4. **Higher-dimensional orbifold topology**: The orbifold {{< m >}}T^n/S_n{{< /m >}} has non-trivial orbifold fundamental group and orbifold Euler characteristic. For {{< m >}}n \geq 4{{< /m >}}, the topology of these spaces is not yet fully classified in music-theoretically useful terms.

{{< conjecture name="Optimality of PLR in Voice-Leading" >}}
Among all involutory chord transformations that preserve two common tones in a triad, PLR uniquely minimizes the maximum single-voice displacement over all 24 triads. No other set of three involutions achieves the same parsimony bound simultaneously for all elements of {{< m >}}\mathcal{T}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /conjecture >}}
