## Overview

Jacob Collier's "Super Ultra Hyper Mega Meta Lydian" is not just a joke. It describes a real mathematical structure: an infinite chain of Lydian tetrachords ascending the circle of fifths, never stopping, never repeating. But on a piano — which uses 12-TET — the spiral is forced into a circle, and after 12 steps you return to the same key.

> *中文:* "圆是钢琴的近似。螺旋是数学的真实。SUM的永不停步，恰好映射了这条螺旋。"

The key mathematical fact is {{< m >}}\gcd(7, 12) = 1{{< /m >}}: the interval of a fifth (7 semitones) generates the entire group {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} because 7 and 12 are coprime. This is why the circle of fifths visits all 12 pitch classes before returning. Any step size {{< m >}}k{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}\gcd(k, 12) > 1{{< /m >}} would only visit a proper subgroup of pitch classes — a smaller cycle, not the full circle.

This episode is the third in the Jacob Collier arc. The spiral/circle duality (EP11's irrational {{< m >}}\log_2(3/2){{< /m >}} forced into rational approximation by 12-TET) is now recast as a topological and algebraic fact: the true pitch space is a helix {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}, the chromatic circle is its quotient {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}, and the circle of fifths is a different traversal of the same quotient.

## Definitions

{{< definition name="Generator of ℤₙ" label="Definition 12.1" >}}
An element {{< m >}}k \in \mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}} is a **generator** of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}} if the cyclic subgroup {{< m >}}\langle k \rangle = \{0, k, 2k, \ldots\} \pmod n{{< /m >}} equals all of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}}.

**Theorem (standard):** {{< m >}}k{{< /m >}} generates {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}} if and only if {{< m >}}\gcd(k, n) = 1{{< /m >}}.

**Proof sketch:** If {{< m >}}d = \gcd(k,n) > 1{{< /m >}}, then {{< m >}}jk \equiv 0 \pmod{d}{{< /m >}} for all {{< m >}}j{{< /m >}}, so {{< m >}}\langle k \rangle \subseteq \{0, d, 2d, \ldots\}{{< /m >}}, a proper subgroup of size {{< m >}}n/d < n{{< /m >}}. Conversely, if {{< m >}}\gcd(k,n)=1{{< /m >}}, Bezout's theorem gives integers {{< m >}}a, b{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}ak + bn = 1{{< /m >}}, so {{< m >}}ak \equiv 1 \pmod n{{< /m >}}, meaning {{< m >}}k{{< /m >}} has a multiplicative inverse and {{< m >}}\langle k \rangle = \mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}}. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Circle of Fifths" label="Definition 12.2" >}}
The **circle of fifths** is the sequence of pitch classes generated by repeated transposition by 7 semitones (a perfect fifth in 12-TET) in {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}:

{{< dm >}}C \to G \to D \to A \to E \to B \to F\sharp \to C\sharp \to A\flat \to E\flat \to B\flat \to F \to C{{< /dm >}}

In {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}: {{< m >}}0 \to 7 \to 2 \to 9 \to 4 \to 11 \to 6 \to 1 \to 8 \to 3 \to 10 \to 5 \to 0{{< /m >}}.

Since {{< m >}}\gcd(7, 12) = 1{{< /m >}} (as 7 is prime and does not divide 12), 7 generates {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}, and the sequence visits all 12 pitch classes before returning to the start. This is the mathematical content of "the circle of fifths is a circle and not a shorter cycle."
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Shepard–Risset Pitch Helix" label="Definition 12.3" >}}
The **Shepard–Risset pitch helix** embeds pitch into {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}^3{{< /m >}} as a helix, capturing both chroma (position on the chromatic circle) and height (octave register):

{{< dm >}}\mathbf{p}(f) = \bigl(r\cos(2\pi \cdot \text{chroma}(f)/12),\; r\sin(2\pi \cdot \text{chroma}(f)/12),\; h \cdot \log_2(f/f_0)\bigr){{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}\text{chroma}(f) = 12\log_2(f/f_0) \bmod 12 \in [0, 12){{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}r > 0{{< /m >}} is the helix radius, {{< m >}}h > 0{{< /m >}} is the vertical pitch height scale, and {{< m >}}f_0{{< /m >}} is a reference frequency.

- **Projection onto the {{< m >}}xy{{< /m >}}-plane** (the horizontal circle): gives chroma only — octave equivalence, the chromatic circle {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}.
- **Projection onto the {{< m >}}z{{< /m >}}-axis** (vertical): gives octave height only — chroma equivalence.
- **The full helix**: represents the two-dimensional structure of pitch: chroma {{< m >}}\times{{< /m >}} register.

A Shepard tone is a superposition of sinusoids at all octaves of a given chroma, with a bell-curve amplitude envelope. It sits at a single point on the chromatic circle but is spread vertically along the helix — hence the paradox of indefinitely ascending/descending pitch without change of perceived height.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Subgroups of ℤ₁₂" label="Definition 12.4" >}}
The subgroups of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} correspond bijectively to the divisors of 12:

| Divisor {{< m >}}d{{< /m >}} | Subgroup | Generator | Musical object |
|-------------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | {{< m >}}\{0\}{{< /m >}} | — | Unison |
| 2 | {{< m >}}\{0, 6\}{{< /m >}} | 6 | Tritone pair |
| 3 | {{< m >}}\{0, 4, 8\}{{< /m >}} | 4 | Augmented triad |
| 4 | {{< m >}}\{0, 3, 6, 9\}{{< /m >}} | 3 | Diminished seventh |
| 6 | {{< m >}}\{0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10\}{{< /m >}} | 2 | Whole-tone scale |
| 12 | {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} | 1 or 7 | Chromatic scale / circle of fifths |

The richness of 12's factor structure ({{< m >}}12 = 2^2 \cdot 3{{< /m >}}, with divisors {{< m >}}\{1,2,3,4,6,12\}{{< /m >}}) directly corresponds to the diversity of symmetric chords in 12-TET: each proper subgroup is a maximally even, symmetric chord or scale.
{{< /definition >}}

## Main Theorems

{{< theorem name="7 Generates ℤ₁₂" label="Theorem 12.1" >}}
The element 7 generates {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}: {{< m >}}\langle 7 \rangle = \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}.

Moreover, the generators of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} are exactly the elements coprime to 12: {{< m >}}\{1, 5, 7, 11\}{{< /m >}}, and {{< m >}}|\text{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{12})| = \phi(12) = 4{{< /m >}}.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
{{< m >}}\gcd(7, 12) = \gcd(7, 12){{< /m >}}. Since {{< m >}}12 = 1 \times 7 + 5{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}7 = 1 \times 5 + 2{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}5 = 2 \times 2 + 1{{< /m >}}, we get {{< m >}}\gcd(7,12) = 1{{< /m >}}. By Definition 12.1, {{< m >}}\langle 7 \rangle = \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}.

The elements coprime to 12 are those not divisible by 2 or 3: {{< m >}}\{1, 5, 7, 11\}{{< /m >}}. Euler's totient {{< m >}}\phi(12) = \phi(4)\phi(3) = 2 \times 2 = 4{{< /m >}} counts these, confirming 4 generators. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}

> *中文:* "7 和 12 的最大公约数等于 1——7 和 12 互质。这保证了 7 能生成 ℤ₁₂ 的全部元素。其他步长，只要和 12 不互质，就只能在一个子集里转圈。只有最大公约数等于 1，才能遍历全部。秘密就是这一个条件。"
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="Circle of Fifths as Quotient of the Spiral" label="Theorem 12.2" >}}
In exact (just) intonation, the pitch space is {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} (the real line of log-frequencies). The "spiral of fifths" is the infinite sequence

{{< dm >}}\ldots, \tfrac{3}{2}\cdot p,\; p,\; \tfrac{3}{2}p,\; \left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^2 p,\; \ldots{{< /dm >}}

which, by Theorem 11.3, never repeats. In 12-TET, this spiral is projected onto the quotient {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/(12\mathbb{Z}){{< /m >}} (identifying pitches that differ by an octave and rounding each fifth to {{< m >}}2^{7/12}{{< /m >}}). The resulting image is exactly {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}, traversed by the generator 7 — the circle of fifths.

Formally, the map {{< m >}}\phi: n \mapsto 7n \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} is the bijection {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} that converts "step number in the fifth sequence" to "pitch class."
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
The projection map is {{< m >}}\pi: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}/(12\mathbb{Z}){{< /m >}}, where we identify {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/(12\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} by {{< m >}}x \mapsto \lfloor x \rfloor \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} (measuring in semitones).

In just intonation, the {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-th fifth above {{< m >}}p{{< /m >}} (at log-frequency 0) is at log-frequency {{< m >}}n \times \log_2(3/2) \approx n \times 0.585{{< /m >}} octaves {{< m >}}= n \times 7.02{{< /m >}} semitones.

In 12-TET, this is rounded to {{< m >}}n \times 7{{< /m >}} semitones. The image {{< m >}}\pi(7n) = 7n \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} is the circle of fifths sequence.

Since {{< m >}}\gcd(7,12)=1{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\phi: n \mapsto 7n \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} is a bijection on {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} (Theorem 12.1), confirming the circle visits all 12 pitch classes. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}

**The key approximation:** The true fifth is {{< m >}}\log_2(3/2) \approx 7.0196{{< /m >}} semitones; 12-TET rounds this to exactly 7. The error is 0.0196 semitones {{< m >}}= 1.955{{< /m >}} cents per fifth. After 12 fifths, the accumulated error is {{< m >}}12 \times 1.955 = 23.46{{< /m >}} cents — the Pythagorean comma. 12-TET distributes this error uniformly, one fifth at a time, forcing the spiral into a circle.
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="Unique Factorization and Good EDOs" label="Theorem 12.3" >}}
An {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-tone equal division of the octave ({{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-EDO) approximates the just fifth if and only if {{< m >}}k = \lfloor n \cdot \log_2(3/2) \rceil{{< /m >}} satisfies {{< m >}}\gcd(k, n) = 1{{< /m >}}, where {{< m >}}\lfloor \cdot \rceil{{< /m >}} denotes the nearest integer. If {{< m >}}\gcd(k,n) > 1{{< /m >}}, the "circle of fifths" in {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-EDO does not generate all {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}} pitch classes.

The best-approximating EDOs with {{< m >}}\gcd(k,n)=1{{< /m >}} are precisely the denominators of the convergents of {{< m >}}\log_2(3/2) = [0;1,1,2,2,3,1,5,2,23,\ldots]{{< /m >}}, namely {{< m >}}n \in \{1, 2, 5, 12, 29, 41, 53, \ldots\}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
For {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-EDO, each step is {{< m >}}1/n{{< /m >}} octave. The fifth is approximated by {{< m >}}k{{< /m >}} steps where {{< m >}}k = \lfloor n\log_2(3/2) \rceil{{< /m >}}. The circle of fifths in {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-EDO generates the subgroup {{< m >}}\langle k \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}}, which equals all of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}} iff {{< m >}}\gcd(k,n)=1{{< /m >}} (Definition 12.1).

For 12-EDO: {{< m >}}k = \lfloor 12 \times 0.58496 \rceil = \lfloor 7.02 \rceil = 7{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\gcd(7,12) = 1{{< /m >}}. Good.

For 5-EDO: {{< m >}}k = \lfloor 5 \times 0.58496 \rceil = \lfloor 2.92 \rceil = 3{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\gcd(3,5) = 1{{< /m >}}. Good (but very coarse).

For 6-EDO: {{< m >}}k = \lfloor 6 \times 0.58496 \rceil = \lfloor 3.51 \rceil = 4{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\gcd(4,6) = 2 \neq 1{{< /m >}}. The "fifth" in 6-EDO only generates a 3-element subgroup — not all 6 pitch classes.

For 53-EDO: {{< m >}}k = \lfloor 53 \times 0.58496 \rceil = \lfloor 31.00 \rceil = 31{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\gcd(31,53) = 1{{< /m >}} (both prime, and {{< m >}}31 \neq 53{{< /m >}}). Good and very accurate ({{< m >}}\approx 0.07{{< /m >}} cents error per fifth). {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proposition label="Prop 12.1" name="Lydian Tetrachord Chain (SUM) as a Coset Sequence" >}}
The Super Ultra Hyper Mega Meta Lydian (SUM) structure of Jacob Collier is the sequence of Lydian tetrachord roots {{< m >}}\{C, G, D, A, E, B, F\sharp, \ldots\}{{< /m >}}, which is the orbit of 0 under repeated addition of 7 in {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} (or {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} before reduction). In 12-TET (the circle), the sequence closes after 12 steps. In just intonation (the spiral), it never closes: each G is a true {{< m >}}(3/2)^n \cdot C{{< /m >}}, lying at a different height on the pitch helix, and the chain is an infinite ascending structure.
{{< /proposition >}}

## Numerical Example: Why 12 and Not 7 or 11?

Consider alternative chromatic universes:

**7-EDO** (7 equal divisions): {{< m >}}k = \lfloor 7 \times 0.585 \rceil = 4{{< /m >}}. {{< m >}}\gcd(4,7)=1{{< /m >}}, so a "fifth" of 4 steps generates all 7 pitch classes. But the fifth is {{< m >}}4/7{{< /m >}} octave {{< m >}}\approx 685.7{{< /m >}} cents — 16 cents flat. No perfect approximation of the major third exists (no {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}4n/7 \approx 386/1200{{< /m >}}). 7-EDO works for pentatonic/heptatonic music but lacks the interval richness of 12-TET.

**12-EDO**: As shown, {{< m >}}\gcd(7,12)=1{{< /m >}}, fifth error {{< m >}}\approx 2{{< /m >}} cents, major third error {{< m >}}\approx 14{{< /m >}} cents. The compromise that Western tonal practice settled on.

**19-EDO**: {{< m >}}k = \lfloor 19 \times 0.585 \rceil = 11{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\gcd(11,19)=1{{< /m >}}. Fifth error {{< m >}}\approx 7{{< /m >}} cents (worse than 12-TET!), but major third {{< m >}}= 6/19{{< /m >}} octave {{< m >}}\approx 378{{< /m >}} cents — 8 cents flat, actually closer to just than 12-TET's 14 cents sharp. Used in some microtonal compositions for its smoother thirds.

**53-EDO**: {{< m >}}k=31{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}\gcd(31,53)=1{{< /m >}}. Fifth error {{< m >}}< 0.1{{< /m >}} cents, major third error {{< m >}}< 0.3{{< /m >}} cents. Nearly perfect 5-limit just intonation, but with 53 keys per octave — impractical for keyboard instruments, though used in some theoretical works (Mercator, 1670s; Tanaka, 1890).

> *中文:* "12不是随意的——它的因子结构恰好对应了西方调性音乐常用的对称数量。24太多——虽然中东音乐在用——7太少，因为它是素数，没有真子群。对于十二平均律体系，12是一个有效的选择。"

## Musical Connection

{{< musical-connection >}}
**The circle of fifths as a group-theoretic object**

The circle of fifths is not merely a mnemonic for key signatures. It is the orbit of any pitch class under the group automorphism {{< m >}}\phi: x \mapsto x + 7 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}}, which has order 12 (since {{< m >}}12 \times 7 \equiv 0 \pmod{12}{{< /m >}} and no smaller multiple works). The circle of fifths and the chromatic scale are two different generating sequences of the same group {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} — one with generator 1 (ascending by semitones), the other with generator 7 (ascending by fifths).

**Tonal brightness as direction on the spiral**

Collier's observation that "going sharp" (adding fifths in the direction {{< m >}}C \to G \to D \to \ldots{{< /m >}}) sounds "brighter" is a perceptual reflection of the acoustic fact that the low-numbered partials of a tone at frequency {{< m >}}f{{< /m >}} are {{< m >}}f, 2f, 3f, \ldots{{< /m >}}. A fifth up means moving toward the 3rd partial, which is acoustically close to the fundamental. A fourth up (adding flats, going {{< m >}}C \to F \to B\flat \to \ldots{{< /m >}}) means moving away from the fundamental's low partials.

> *中文:* "为什么五度方向更亮？纯律五度等于三比二——就是泛音列的第3个分音。加7方向就是沿着低序数泛音堆叠，和基音更亲近。明与暗，就是你在五度圈上的方向。"

**The Shepard–Risset paradox and musical meaning**

Shepard tones (Definition 12.3) exploit the helix structure: by spreading amplitude across all octaves of a chroma with a bell envelope, they create the illusion of a tone with chroma but no register — a "pure" position on the chromatic circle without height. Shepard-Risset glissandi create the paradox of continuous ascent without arriving higher — the chromatic circle rotates continuously, but the vertical helix position stays fixed.

Collier's SUM is the philosophical inverse: it insists on the helix, refusing the projection. On a piano (which forces {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}), SUM closes after 12 steps. In just intonation, or in the voice (which can access any frequency), it never closes — it ascends the spiral indefinitely.

> *中文:* "五度圈是一个优美的谎言——代价是每个五度损失2音分的真实，收益是12个调式的平等。正是这个谎言，让转调成为可能。"

**Subgroup structure and symmetric chords**

Definition 12.4 shows that {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}'s subgroups correspond to the most structurally symmetric objects in 12-TET: the tritone pair, augmented triad, diminished seventh chord, and whole-tone scale. These are exactly the chords whose transpositional symmetry makes them "tonally unstable" — they sound unresolved because they belong equally to multiple keys. The augmented triad is the unique chord invariant under transposition by a major third ({{< m >}}T_4{{< /m >}}); the diminished seventh is invariant under {{< m >}}T_3{{< /m >}}. Their ambiguity is a direct consequence of being proper subgroups of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /musical-connection >}}

## Limits and Open Questions

1. **Multidimensional pitch spaces.** The Shepard helix is one-dimensional in chroma (a circle) with one height dimension. Krumhansl's (1983) multidimensional scaling experiments suggest that the perceptual space of keys is better modeled as a **torus** (circle of major keys × circle of minor keys) embedded in a higher-dimensional space. The torus is the product of two {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}} circles (one major, one minor), connected by relative and parallel relationships. This structure cannot be captured by a single helix.

2. **Optimal EDOs for extended just intonation.** Theorem 12.3 characterizes good EDOs for 5-limit just intonation (primes 2, 3, 5). For 7-limit (adding 7:4), the optimal EDOs change: 31-EDO and 72-EDO become attractive. The general question — "what is the best {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-EDO for {{< m >}}p{{< /m >}}-limit just intonation?" — is an active research area in mathematical music theory.

3. **The SUM structure in continuous pitch space.** Collier's SUM, if extended indefinitely in just intonation, is an infinite ascending sequence in {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} (the pitch helix). The question of whether a composition can make musical sense while its tonal center spirals to arbitrarily high pitch is open. Pauline Oliveros's deep listening work and La Monte Young's sustained-tone pieces approach this question experientially, but no formal compositional framework for "infinite spiral form" exists.

4. **Tuning and spectral composition.** Sethares (2005) showed that inharmonic timbres can be made consonant with non-12-TET scales by matching the instrument's partial series to the EDO's intervals. This raises the converse question: if the "circle of fifths" and the subgroup structure of {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_n{{< /m >}} depend on {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}, and the consonance of intervals depends on the timbre, is there a joint optimization of ({{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}-EDO, timbre) that produces a richer harmonic world than 12-TET with harmonic timbres?
