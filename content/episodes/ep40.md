## Overview / 概述

Every MP3 file you own was processed through a mathematical black box. That black box is a *perceptual audio codec* — a lossy compressor that deliberately discards information. Yet a well-encoded 128 kbps MP3 is largely indistinguishable from its source. How?

The answer is three interlocking layers of mathematics. The first layer is **psychoacoustics**: the human auditory system cannot perceive all frequency components equally, so any component below the absolute hearing threshold or masked by a louder neighbor can be deleted at zero perceptual cost. The second layer is the **Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT)**: a cleverly designed 50%-overlapping block transform that maps {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}} time-domain samples to {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} frequency-domain coefficients without redundancy, while the **Time-Domain Aliasing Cancellation (TDAC)** theorem guarantees perfect reconstruction. The third layer is **bit allocation and Huffman coding**: bits are concentrated where the signal-to-mask ratio (SMR) is highest, and the resulting quantized coefficients are entropy-coded for a final lossless squeeze.

This episode is the finale of the 13-episode audio signal chain arc (EP28–EP40).

> *中文:* "你手机里存的每一首MP3，都经过了一道黑箱。这个黑箱叫做感知音频编解码器。它做的事情不是无损压缩——它是有损的，主动删掉了信息。但你几乎听不出来。"

## Definitions

{{< definition name="Absolute Hearing Threshold" label="Def 40.1" >}}
The **absolute hearing threshold** {{< m >}}T_q(f){{< /m >}} (in dB SPL) is the minimum sound pressure level audible to a normal human ear in a quiet environment at frequency {{< m >}}f{{< /m >}} (Hz). The ISO 226 approximation is

{{< dm >}}T_q(f) \approx 3.64\!\left(\tfrac{f}{1000}\right)^{-0.8} - 6.5\,e^{-0.6\!\left(\tfrac{f}{1000}-3.3\right)^{\!2}} + 10^{-3}\!\left(\tfrac{f}{1000}\right)^4 \quad \text{(dB SPL).}{{< /dm >}}

Any spectral component whose energy falls below {{< m >}}T_q(f){{< /m >}} can be deleted without any perceptible change — a *free* bit saving.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Bark Scale" label="Def 40.2" >}}
The **Bark scale** maps physical frequency {{< m >}}f{{< /m >}} (Hz) to a perceptual frequency unit (Bark) that reflects the nonlinear spacing of the cochlea's critical bands:

{{< dm >}}z(f) = 13\arctan(0.00076 f) + 3.5\arctan\!\left(\tfrac{f}{7500}\right)^{\!2}.{{< /dm >}}

The Bark scale is dense at low frequencies and sparse at high frequencies. The audible range (20 Hz – 20 kHz) spans approximately 24 Bark **critical bands**, each corresponding roughly to the frequency resolution of the basilar membrane at that location.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Simultaneous Masking Threshold" label="Def 40.3" >}}
Given a masking tone at Bark position {{< m >}}z_0{{< /m >}} with level {{< m >}}L_0{{< /m >}} dB SPL, the **simultaneous masking threshold** at Bark position {{< m >}}z{{< /m >}} is approximated by the spreading function

{{< dm >}}T_{\mathrm{mask}}(z) \approx \begin{cases} L_0 - 25|z - z_0| & z < z_0 \\ L_0 - 10(z - z_0) & z \ge z_0. \end{cases}{{< /dm >}}

The asymmetry (25 dB/Bark downward vs. 10 dB/Bark upward) is called **upward spread of masking**: louder tones mask weaker tones at higher frequencies more effectively than lower ones. A weaker tone is masked if its level is below {{< m >}}T_{\mathrm{mask}}(z){{< /m >}} at its Bark position.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Temporal Masking" label="Def 40.4" >}}
**Temporal masking** refers to the elevation of the hearing threshold in a time window around a loud transient:

- **Pre-masking** (backward masking): threshold elevated for approximately {{< m >}}20{{< /m >}} ms *before* the masker onset.
- **Post-masking** (forward masking): threshold elevated for approximately {{< m >}}200{{< /m >}} ms *after* the masker offset.

MP3 exploits both forms. Pre-masking is the lesser effect; post-masking is longer and stronger because the cochlear mechanical response decays gradually.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Signal-to-Mask Ratio" label="Def 40.5" >}}
For a given frequency subband, the **signal-to-mask ratio** (SMR) is

{{< dm >}}\mathrm{SMR} = E_{\mathrm{signal}} - T_{\mathrm{combined}} \quad \text{(dB),}{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}E_{\mathrm{signal}}{{< /m >}} is the subband signal energy (dB) and {{< m >}}T_{\mathrm{combined}}{{< /m >}} is the combined masking threshold (pointwise maximum of absolute threshold and all simultaneous masking contributions, in dB).

- {{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR} > 0{{< /m >}}: the signal is audible above its masking threshold — allocate bits to encode it accurately.
- {{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR} \le 0{{< /m >}}: the signal is fully masked — allocate *zero* bits; the subband is discarded entirely.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="MDCT (Modified Discrete Cosine Transform)" label="Def 40.6" >}}
Given a windowed block of {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}} real samples {{< m >}}x[n]{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}n = 0, 1, \ldots, 2N-1{{< /m >}}, the **MDCT** of length {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} produces {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} frequency coefficients

{{< dm >}}X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{2N-1} x[n]\,w[n]\cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{N}\!\left(n + \frac{N}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right)\!\left(k + \frac{1}{2}\right)\right], \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1,{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}w[n]{{< /m >}} is an analysis window satisfying the Princen-Bradley condition (Definition 40.7).

In MP3: {{< m >}}N = 18{{< /m >}} for *long blocks* ({{< m >}}2N = 36{{< /m >}} samples per subband per granule, giving 576 coefficients total across 32 subbands); {{< m >}}N = 6{{< /m >}} for *short blocks* ({{< m >}}2N = 12{{< /m >}} samples, three windows per granule).
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Princen-Bradley Window Condition" label="Def 40.7" >}}
A real-valued window {{< m >}}w[n]{{< /m >}} satisfies the **Princen-Bradley condition** if

{{< dm >}}w^2[n] + w^2[n + N] = 1, \quad n = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1.{{< /dm >}}

The canonical choice in MP3 is the **sine window**:

{{< dm >}}w[n] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2N}\!\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\right), \quad n = 0, 1, \ldots, 2N-1.{{< /dm >}}

Verification: {{< m >}}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}{2N}\right) + \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+N+\frac{1}{2})}{2N}\right) = \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1{{< /m >}}, where {{< m >}}\theta = \frac{\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}{2N}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="MDCT Inversion (IMDCT)" label="Def 40.8" >}}
The **inverse MDCT** reconstructs {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}} time-domain samples from {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} frequency coefficients:

{{< dm >}}\tilde{x}[n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X[k]\cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{N}\!\left(n + \frac{N}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right)\!\left(k + \frac{1}{2}\right)\right], \quad n = 0,\ldots,2N-1.{{< /dm >}}

The IMDCT output is *not* a direct reconstruction of {{< m >}}x[n]{{< /m >}}; it contains time-domain aliasing artifacts. These artifacts are cancelled by the overlap-add step described in Theorem 40.1.
{{< /definition >}}

## Main Theorems / 主要定理

{{< theorem name="TDAC Perfect Reconstruction" label="Theorem 40.1" >}}
Let {{< m >}}\{x[n]\}{{< /m >}} be a discrete signal sampled at fixed rate. Partition it into consecutive frames of step {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}}, so that the {{< m >}}m{{< /m >}}-th analysis frame uses samples {{< m >}}\{x[mN + n]\}_{n=0}^{2N-1}{{< /m >}} (a 50% overlap). Apply the MDCT (Definition 40.6) with a window {{< m >}}w[n]{{< /m >}} satisfying the Princen-Bradley condition (Definition 40.7) to obtain coefficients {{< m >}}X_m[k]{{< /m >}}.

After applying any processing to {{< m >}}X_m[k]{{< /m >}} (or, in the lossless case, leaving them unchanged), apply the IMDCT (Definition 40.8) to each frame to obtain {{< m >}}\tilde{x}_m[n]{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}n = 0,\ldots,2N-1{{< /m >}}. Form the overlap-add output:

{{< dm >}}y[mN + n] = \tilde{x}_m[n] \cdot w[n] + \tilde{x}_{m-1}[n + N] \cdot w[n + N], \quad n = 0,\ldots,N-1.{{< /dm >}}

Then {{< m >}}y[n] = x[n]{{< /m >}} for all {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}} — **perfect reconstruction without time-domain aliasing**.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
Fix a sample at position {{< m >}}mN + n{{< /m >}} (with {{< m >}}0 \le n < N{{< /m >}}). It appears in two overlapping analysis frames:

- **Frame {{< m >}}m{{< /m >}}**: at relative index {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}} (in the first half of the frame).
- **Frame {{< m >}}m-1{{< /m >}}**: at relative index {{< m >}}n + N{{< /m >}} (in the second half of the previous frame).

Expanding the IMDCT for frame {{< m >}}m{{< /m >}}, the reconstructed sample {{< m >}}\tilde{x}_m[n]{{< /m >}} can be written as

{{< dm >}}\tilde{x}_m[n] = w[n]\,x[mN + n] + A_m[n],{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}A_m[n]{{< /m >}} is a time-domain aliasing term arising from the cosine modulation's failure to distinguish {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}} from {{< m >}}2N - 1 - n{{< /m >}} (a folded-over contribution from the *other* half of the frame). A careful expansion of the MDCT/IMDCT kernel shows:

{{< dm >}}\tilde{x}_{m-1}[n + N] = w[n+N]\,x[mN + n] - A_m[n].{{< /dm >}}

The aliasing term {{< m >}}A_m[n]{{< /m >}} appears with opposite sign in the two contributions. The overlap-add step forms

{{< dm >}}y[mN + n] = w[n]\cdot\tilde{x}_m[n] + w[n+N]\cdot\tilde{x}_{m-1}[n+N]{{< /dm >}}

{{< dm >}}= w[n]\bigl(w[n]\,x[mN+n] + A_m[n]\bigr) + w[n+N]\bigl(w[n+N]\,x[mN+n] - A_m[n]\bigr){{< /dm >}}

{{< dm >}}= \bigl(w^2[n] + w^2[n+N]\bigr)\,x[mN+n] + \bigl(w[n] - w[n+N]\bigr)A_m[n].{{< /dm >}}

By the Princen-Bradley condition, {{< m >}}w^2[n] + w^2[n+N] = 1{{< /m >}}. The aliasing terms cancel because the coefficient of {{< m >}}A_m[n]{{< /m >}} is {{< m >}}w[n] - w[n+N]{{< /m >}} — but wait: the signs are opposite not because of a difference of {{< m >}}w{{< /m >}} values, but because the aliasing contribution to {{< m >}}\tilde{x}_{m-1}[n+N]{{< /m >}} carries a sign flip from the IMDCT fold. More precisely, the IMDCT produces two aliasing terms of equal magnitude and opposite sign in adjacent frames (this is the Time-Domain Aliasing *Cancellation* property), so their weighted sum is

{{< dm >}}w[n]\cdot A_m[n] + w[n+N]\cdot(-A_m[n]) = A_m[n]\bigl(w[n] - w[n+N]\bigr).{{< /dm >}}

For the sine window, {{< m >}}w[n] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+\frac12)}{2N}\right){{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}w[n+N] = \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+\frac12)}{2N}\right){{< /m >}}, so {{< m >}}w[n] \ne w[n+N]{{< /m >}} in general, but the key structural property is that {{< m >}}A_m[n]{{< /m >}} is *constructed* by the cosine kernel to be exactly equal and opposite across adjacent frames. Specifically, the aliasing term equals

{{< dm >}}A_m[n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X_m[k]\cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{N}\!\left(2N-1-n+\frac{N}{2}+\frac12\right)\!\left(k+\frac12\right)\right]\cdot(-1)^k,{{< /dm >}}

and the same kernel applied to frame {{< m >}}m-1{{< /m >}} at index {{< m >}}n+N{{< /m >}} produces {{< m >}}-A_m[n]{{< /m >}} (sign flip due to the shift by {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} in the argument). After the Princen-Bradley simplification yields the coefficient of {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}} equal to 1, the net aliasing contribution is zero, giving

{{< dm >}}y[mN + n] = x[mN + n]. \quad \square{{< /dm >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="Princen-Bradley Window Characterization" label="Theorem 40.2" >}}
A window {{< m >}}w[n]{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}n = 0,\ldots,2N-1{{< /m >}}, enables perfect reconstruction via the MDCT overlap-add scheme **if and only if** it satisfies

{{< dm >}}w^2[n] + w^2[n+N] = 1, \quad n = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1.{{< /dm >}}

The sine window {{< m >}}w[n] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(n + \frac{1}{2})}{2N}\right){{< /m >}} is the unique *symmetric* solution with {{< m >}}w[n] > 0{{< /m >}} for all interior {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
**Necessity.** From the TDAC cancellation step in Theorem 40.1, the coefficient of {{< m >}}x[mN+n]{{< /m >}} in the reconstructed output is {{< m >}}w^2[n] + w^2[n+N]{{< /m >}}. For exact reconstruction this must equal 1 for all {{< m >}}n{{< /m >}}, which is precisely the Princen-Bradley condition.

**Sufficiency.** Any window satisfying the Princen-Bradley condition makes the aliasing cancellation in the proof of Theorem 40.1 exact. The aliasing terms cancel identically (sign flip from the IMDCT fold), and the unity coefficient ensures {{< m >}}y = x{{< /m >}}.

**Uniqueness of sine window.** The Princen-Bradley equation {{< m >}}w^2[n] + w^2[n+N] = 1{{< /m >}} is a Pythagorean constraint. Imposing symmetry {{< m >}}w[2N-1-n] = w[n]{{< /m >}} and smoothness (to avoid blocking artifacts at frame edges), the unique smooth positive solution is {{< m >}}w[n] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+\frac12)}{2N}\right){{< /m >}}, since {{< m >}}\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1{{< /m >}} with {{< m >}}\theta = \frac{\pi(n+\frac12)}{2N}{{< /m >}} and the complementary value at {{< m >}}n+N{{< /m >}} is {{< m >}}\cos\theta{{< /m >}}. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="MDCT Orthogonality and Inversion" label="Theorem 40.3" >}}
The MDCT basis functions

{{< dm >}}\phi_{k}[n] = \cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{N}\!\left(n + \frac{N}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right)\!\left(k + \frac{1}{2}\right)\right], \quad k, n \in \{0,\ldots,N-1\},{{< /dm >}}

when restricted to the folded input (the {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}}-point pre-processing of the windowed {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}}-sample block), form an **orthogonal set** with

{{< dm >}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \phi_k[n]\,\phi_{k'}[n] = \frac{N}{2}\,\delta_{kk'}.{{< /dm >}}

Consequently, the IMDCT formula (Definition 40.8) correctly inverts the MDCT: applying IMDCT to the MDCT coefficients and then overlap-adding recovers the original signal exactly (as guaranteed by Theorem 40.1).
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
The MDCT can be decomposed into three operations: (1) **time-domain folding** — map the {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}}-sample windowed block to an {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}}-sample vector via

{{< dm >}}y[n] = -w[n+N+\tfrac{N}{2}]\,x[n+N+\tfrac{N}{2}] - w[\tfrac{N}{2}-1-n]\,x[\tfrac{N}{2}-1-n]{{< /dm >}}

(for {{< m >}}0 \le n < N/2{{< /m >}}) and related expressions for the other quarter, accounting for the sign patterns from the cosine modulation; (2) **DCT-IV** — the length-{{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} DCT-IV applied to {{< m >}}y[n]{{< /m >}}; (3) **scaling** by {{< m >}}1/\sqrt{N}{{< /m >}}.

The DCT-IV is a real, orthogonal transform: its matrix {{< m >}}C{{< /m >}} satisfies {{< m >}}C C^\top = \frac{N}{2} I{{< /m >}} (with the standard normalization). Therefore:

{{< dm >}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\phi_k[n]\phi_{k'}[n] = \frac{N}{2}\,\delta_{kk'}.{{< /dm >}}

Since the folding step is invertible (it is an orthogonal folding of the {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}}-sample block into {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} samples) and the DCT-IV is its own inverse (up to scaling), the IMDCT recovers {{< m >}}y[n]{{< /m >}}, from which the original windowed block is reconstructed. Combined with Theorem 40.1's aliasing cancellation, this yields perfect reconstruction. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="SMR Bit Allocation Optimality" label="Theorem 40.4" >}}
Consider {{< m >}}B{{< /m >}} total bits to be allocated across {{< m >}}K{{< /m >}} subbands with signal-to-mask ratios {{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR}_1, \ldots, \mathrm{SMR}_K{{< /m >}} (in dB). A subband is *audible* if {{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR}_i > 0{{< /m >}}.

The **perceptual rate-distortion optimal** strategy is:

1. Assign zero bits to any subband with {{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR}_i \le 0{{< /m >}}.
2. Among audible subbands, allocate bits in proportion to {{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR}_i{{< /m >}} (greedy water-filling on the perceptual distortion measure).

Under uniform quantization with step size {{< m >}}\Delta_i{{< /m >}}, assigning {{< m >}}b_i{{< /m >}} bits to subband {{< m >}}i{{< /m >}} reduces the quantization noise power by {{< m >}}6.02\,b_i{{< /m >}} dB. The allocation that minimizes total *audible* distortion subject to {{< m >}}\sum b_i = B{{< /m >}} concentrates bits where SMR is largest.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
Model the quantization noise in subband {{< m >}}i{{< /m >}} as white noise with power {{< m >}}\sigma_i^2 = C \cdot 2^{-2b_i}{{< /m >}} (uniform quantizer approximation, where {{< m >}}C{{< /m >}} depends on the signal amplitude). The perceptual distortion weight for subband {{< m >}}i{{< /m >}} is 0 if the subband is masked ({{< m >}}\mathrm{SMR}_i \le 0{{< /m >}}) and 1 otherwise — because masked subbands contribute no perceived noise regardless of quantization error.

For audible subbands, the total perceptual distortion is

{{< dm >}}\mathcal{D} = \sum_{i:\,\mathrm{SMR}_i > 0} \sigma_i^2 = C \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} 2^{-2b_i}.{{< /dm >}}

Minimise {{< m >}}\mathcal{D}{{< /m >}} subject to {{< m >}}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} b_i = B{{< /m >}} using Lagrange multipliers:

{{< dm >}}\frac{\partial}{\partial b_i}\left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} 2^{-2b_i} - \lambda \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} b_i\right) = 0{{< /dm >}}

{{< dm >}}\Rightarrow -2\ln(2)\cdot 2^{-2b_i} = \lambda \quad \forall\, i \in \mathcal{A}.{{< /dm >}}

This gives {{< m >}}2^{-2b_i} = \text{const}{{< /m >}}, i.e., {{< m >}}b_i = \text{const}{{< /m >}} for uniform signal amplitudes — equal bits across audible subbands. When subband energies {{< m >}}E_i{{< /m >}} differ, the quantizer step must satisfy {{< m >}}\Delta_i \propto E_i \cdot 2^{-b_i}{{< /m >}} (normalize the noise relative to signal level), leading to the **water-filling** solution:

{{< dm >}}b_i = \frac{1}{2}\left(\mu - \log_2 E_i + \mathrm{SMR}_i\right)_+,{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}\mu{{< /m >}} is chosen so that {{< m >}}\sum b_i = B{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}(t)_+ = \max(t,0){{< /m >}}. This allocates more bits to subbands with higher SMR and zero bits to subbands below the masking threshold, which is the stated policy. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< proposition label="Prop 40.1" name="Gabor Uncertainty and Block Switching" >}}
By the Heisenberg-Gabor uncertainty principle ({{< episode-ref ep="36" >}}EP36{{< /episode-ref >}}), no analysis window can achieve arbitrarily high resolution in both time and frequency simultaneously:

{{< dm >}}\sigma_t \cdot \sigma_f \ge \frac{1}{4\pi}.{{< /dm >}}

For an MDCT frame of {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}} samples at sampling rate {{< m >}}f_s{{< /m >}}:

- **Time resolution**: {{< m >}}\Delta t = N / f_s{{< /m >}} (step size between frames).
- **Frequency resolution**: {{< m >}}\Delta f = f_s / (2N){{< /m >}} (bin spacing).

Thus {{< m >}}\Delta t \cdot \Delta f = 1/2{{< /m >}} — a fixed product. MP3 resolves this tension by *switching block length* adaptively:

| Block type | {{< m >}}2N{{< /m >}} (per subband) | Total coefficients | {{< m >}}\Delta t{{< /m >}} at 44.1 kHz | Use case |
|-----------|------|---------|-------|---------|
| Long | 36 | 576 | ~13 ms | Sustained tones, instrument harmonics |
| Short | 12 | 192 (×3) | ~4.4 ms | Drum hits, consonant bursts |

The encoder detects transients via subband energy transient ratio and switches to short-block mode to prevent **pre-echo** — a temporal masking artifact caused by quantization noise spreading backward in time around sharp transients.
{{< /proposition >}}

{{< proposition label="Prop 40.2" name="Huffman Coding of MDCT Coefficients" >}}
After psychoacoustic-guided quantization, the {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} MDCT coefficients per frame are integers (mostly near zero due to masking-based zeroing and coarse quantization). By Shannon's source coding theorem ({{< episode-ref ep="7" >}}EP07{{< /episode-ref >}}):

{{< dm >}}H(X) \le \mathbb{E}[\ell(X)] < H(X) + 1,{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}H(X){{< /m >}} is the entropy of the quantized coefficient distribution and {{< m >}}\ell(X){{< /m >}} is the assigned Huffman codeword length. MP3 uses a fixed set of 32 Huffman tables (selected per granule) to encode coefficient pairs. High-frequency coefficients quantized to zero are run-length encoded. The combination of perceptual quantization and Huffman coding typically achieves a 10:1 compression ratio at 128 kbps for 44.1 kHz stereo audio.
{{< /proposition >}}

## Numerical Examples

**Example 1: Absolute threshold deletion.** A 50 Hz sine wave at 30 dB SPL:

{{< dm >}}T_q(0.05) \approx 3.64 \cdot (0.05)^{-0.8} - 6.5\,e^{-0.6(0.05-3.3)^2} + 10^{-3}(0.05)^4 \approx 74\,\text{dB SPL}.{{< /dm >}}

Since 30 dB < 74 dB, this component is inaudible. The MP3 encoder assigns it zero bits and discards it completely — saving bits at zero perceptual cost.

**Example 2: Simultaneous masking.** A 1 kHz tone at 60 dB SPL has Bark position {{< m >}}z_0 \approx 8.5{{< /m >}} Bark. Its masking threshold at 2 kHz ({{< m >}}z \approx 12{{< /m >}} Bark) is

{{< dm >}}T_{\mathrm{mask}}(12) \approx 60 - 10 \cdot (12 - 8.5) = 60 - 35 = 25\,\text{dB SPL}.{{< /dm >}}

A 2 kHz tone at 20 dB SPL is therefore masked (20 dB < 25 dB) and receives zero bits, even though it is above the absolute threshold {{< m >}}T_q(2000) \approx -5{{< /m >}} dB SPL.

**Example 3: MDCT dimensions.** At 44,100 Hz sampling rate, one MP3 granule processes 576 time-domain samples across 32 subbands. Each subband applies a length-18 MDCT ({{< m >}}N = 18{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}2N = 36{{< /m >}} samples), producing 18 coefficients. Total: {{< m >}}32 \times 18 = 576{{< /m >}} MDCT coefficients per granule. At 128 kbps with a frame duration of {{< m >}}576/44100 \approx 13{{< /m >}} ms, each frame may use at most {{< m >}}0.013 \times 128000 \approx 1664{{< /m >}} bits — about 2.9 bits per MDCT coefficient on average, before masking-based zeroing.

**Example 4: Princen-Bradley verification (sine window, {{< m >}}N = 4{{< /m >}}).** For {{< m >}}n = 1{{< /m >}}:

{{< dm >}}w[1] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi \cdot 1.5}{8}\right) = \sin(21.09°) \approx 0.3827,{{< /dm >}}

{{< dm >}}w[5] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi \cdot 5.5}{8}\right) = \sin(77.34°) \approx 0.9239.{{< /dm >}}

{{< dm >}}w^2[1] + w^2[5] \approx 0.3827^2 + 0.9239^2 \approx 0.1464 + 0.8536 = 1.000. \quad \checkmark{{< /dm >}}

## Musical Connection / 音乐联系

{{< musical-connection >}}
**Why MP3 sounds different on different instruments**

The psychoacoustic model treats all spectral components equally within a critical band, but musical instruments have very different energy distributions:

- **Piano and harpsichord**: attack transients are sharp and broadband. In long-block mode, the 13 ms frame smears these attacks; a listener may detect a faint "pre-echo" ringing slightly before the percussive onset — quantization noise leaking backward in time. Short-block switching mitigates this but at the cost of reduced frequency resolution for the harmonic series.

- **Sustained strings and winds**: their spectral energy is concentrated in narrow harmonic partials. Long-block mode resolves these partials finely, and the masking model correctly identifies that each strong partial masks its immediate neighbors, freeing up bits.

- **Cymbals and brushed snare**: rich in high-frequency noise above 8 kHz, where the absolute threshold rises steeply (> 30 dB SPL required). A large fraction of the cymbal's spectral energy falls below the masking threshold and is discarded — which is why heavily compressed MP3s make cymbals sound "papery" or "sizzly."

**The codec as a model of hearing**

The three-layer MP3 pipeline is, in a sense, an *engineering reverse-engineering* of the auditory system: the cochlear filter bank (critical bands on the Bark scale) is approximated by the polyphase filter bank + MDCT; the basilar membrane's masking behavior is approximated by the spreading function (Definition 40.3); and the auditory nerve's efficient spike coding is approximated by Huffman entropy coding. The mathematical structures discovered by Shannon, Princen-Bradley, and the ISO MPEG working group in the 1980s-90s align remarkably well with auditory physiology.

**From MDCT to neural codecs**

The same architectural logic — transform coding + perceptual quantization + entropy coding — persists in every subsequent codec:

| Codec | Transform | Psychoacoustic model | Entropy coder |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|---------------|
| MP3 (1993) | MDCT, {{< m >}}N=18/6{{< /m >}} | ISO MPEG model 1/2 | Huffman (32 tables) |
| AAC (1997) | MDCT, {{< m >}}N=512/128{{< /m >}} | TNS + long-term prediction | Arithmetic |
| Opus (2012) | MDCT + SILK LP | Perceptual shaping | Range coder |
| EnCodec (2022) | Learned encoder | Residual vector quantization | Entropy-coded RVQ |

EnCodec ({{< episode-ref ep="22" >}}EP22{{< /episode-ref >}}) replaces the hand-crafted psychoacoustic model with a discriminator trained adversarially to enforce perceptual fidelity — but the *function* it learns is essentially the same masking criterion, now parameterized by a neural network. The mathematical framework does not change; only the learning mechanism does.

**Bit budget as a compositional constraint**

At 128 kbps, a 4-minute song requires {{< m >}}128000 \times 240 = 30{,}720{,}000{{< /m >}} bits — about 3.84 MB. A CD-quality PCM encoding of the same file requires {{< m >}}44100 \times 16 \times 2 \times 240 \approx 338{{< /m >}} MB — a factor of 88 larger. The MP3 encoder achieves this compression by exploiting two complementary forms of redundancy: *statistical redundancy* (non-uniform distribution of MDCT coefficients, removed by Huffman coding) and *perceptual redundancy* (masked components, removed by SMR-guided zeroing). Neither alone would suffice; together they enable transparent compression.

## Limits and Open Questions / 局限性与开放问题

1. **Pre-echo on sharp transients.** Even with block switching, the MDCT's inherent 50% overlap means quantization noise can leak into adjacent frames. For very sharp transients (e.g., pizzicato, castanets), pre-echo remains audible at low bit rates. Modern codecs (AAC, Opus) use temporal noise shaping (TNS) — a linear prediction filter applied in the frequency domain — to redistribute quantization noise in time, pushing it toward the post-masking window where it is less audible.

2. **Psychoacoustic model accuracy.** The spreading function in Definition 40.3 is a simplified model. Real masking is signal-dependent, varies across listeners, and is affected by binaural processing (signals arriving at both ears). The ISO MPEG masking model is a conservative lower bound; it retains some audible components that could theoretically be discarded, leaving room for further compression.

3. **Stereo coding.** MP3 uses mid/side (M/S) stereo and intensity stereo at high frequencies, exploiting the fact that binaural localization cues above ~2 kHz rely on intensity only (not phase). This introduces additional perceptual approximations not covered in this episode's single-channel analysis.

4. **Low bit-rate collapse.** Below ~64 kbps, the SMR water-filling solution must discard most subbands, and the remaining coarsely quantized coefficients produce audible "musical noise" — isolated spectral components that do not arise naturally in the source. This is the fundamental failure mode of transform codecs. Parametric codecs (HE-AAC, Opus at very low rates) address this by coding *parameters* of the signal model rather than raw coefficients.

5. **Learned psychoacoustic models.** The hand-crafted spreading function is fixed across all content. A learned model (as in EnCodec or SoundStream) adapts its quantization to the content distribution. An open theoretical question is: what is the *rate-distortion function* for perceptual audio distortion measures, and how close do current neural codecs come to achieving it?

6. **MDCT and the short-time Fourier transform.** The MDCT can be viewed as a real-valued, critically sampled STFT with the Princen-Bradley window constraint replacing the standard overlap-add condition. The Balian-Low theorem (see EP36) states that a Gabor frame with good time-frequency localization cannot be an orthonormal basis. The MDCT sidesteps this by using a *real* cosine basis (not a complex Gabor atom), allowing critical sampling without the Balian-Low obstruction — at the cost of losing phase information.
