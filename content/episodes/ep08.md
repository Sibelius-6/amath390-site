## Overview

A tone rises through twelve semitones. Then it rises again. It has been rising for thirty seconds and is at exactly the same pitch it started on.

This is not an acoustic trick — it is a topological fact. Roger Shepard's 1964 construction exploits the two-dimensional structure of human pitch perception: every pitch has a **chroma** (position on a circle, {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}) and a **height** (octave number, {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}). Pitch lives on a helix {{< m >}}S^1 \times \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}. By smearing the spectral energy uniformly across octaves with a bell-shaped envelope, Shepard erases the height coordinate, leaving only the chroma circle {{< m >}}S^1 \cong \mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}.

Once height is ambiguous, the tritone (six semitones, diametrically opposite on the chroma circle) has no objective "direction." Diana Deutsch's 1986 experiments revealed that listeners consistently hear the same Shepard tritone pair as ascending *or* descending depending on their linguistic background — specifically the tonal range of their native language. Your perception fills the missing dimension with cultural prior information.

Mathematically this episode covers: quotient topology, the universal cover of {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}}, and a capsule account of spherical harmonics for 3D audio.

## Definitions

{{< definition name="Pitch Helix" label="Def 8.1" >}}
The **pitch helix** is the topological space

{{< dm >}}\mathcal{H} = S^1 \times \mathbb{R}{{< /dm >}}

parameterized by:
- **Chroma** {{< m >}}\theta \in S^1 \cong \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}: the pitch class modulo octave equivalence, with {{< m >}}\theta = k/12{{< /m >}} for semitone {{< m >}}k \in \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}
- **Height** {{< m >}}h \in \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}: the octave number (e.g., {{< m >}}h = 4{{< /m >}} for the octave containing middle C)

A physical tone with fundamental frequency {{< m >}}f{{< /m >}} maps to the point with chroma {{< m >}}\theta = \{12 \log_2(f/f_{\text{ref}})\}/12{{< /m >}} (fractional part, giving position on the circle) and height {{< m >}}h = \lfloor \log_2(f/f_{\text{ref}}) \rfloor{{< /m >}}.

The helix projects onto two quotient spaces:
- Onto {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} (chroma circle) by forgetting height: {{< m >}}(\theta, h) \mapsto \theta{{< /m >}}
- Onto {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} (height line) by forgetting chroma: {{< m >}}(\theta, h) \mapsto h{{< /m >}}
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Shepard Tone" label="Def 8.2" >}}
A **Shepard tone** at chroma {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}} is the superposition of pure sinusoids at all octaves of the corresponding pitch, weighted by a fixed bell-shaped (Gaussian) spectral envelope {{< m >}}A: \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]{{< /m >}} centered at a fixed reference height {{< m >}}h_0{{< /m >}}:

{{< dm >}}S_\theta(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} A(n + h(\theta)) \cdot \sin\!\bigl(2\pi \cdot f_{\text{ref}} \cdot 2^{n + \theta} \cdot t\bigr){{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}h(\theta){{< /m >}} selects the octave closest to the center of the envelope.

The crucial property: the envelope {{< m >}}A{{< /m >}} is **the same for every chroma {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}}**. Moving from {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}} to {{< m >}}\theta + 1/12{{< /m >}} (one semitone) shifts every component up by one semitone: the highest partial fades out, a new lowest partial fades in. The overall spectral shape is preserved.
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Tritone" label="Def 8.3" >}}
A **tritone** is the interval of six semitones — exactly half an octave. On the chroma circle {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}}, two pitches {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\theta + 1/2{{< /m >}} are **antipodal**: they are diametrically opposite, equidistant in both the clockwise and counterclockwise directions.

The tritone was called *diabolus in musica* (the devil in music) in medieval counterpoint and was forbidden in strict voice-leading (see EP05).
{{< /definition >}}

{{< definition name="Quotient Topology and Universal Cover" label="Def 8.4" >}}
The **chroma circle** {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} is the quotient space {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} (the real line with integers identified): two real numbers {{< m >}}x{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}y{{< /m >}} are identified iff {{< m >}}x - y \in \mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}.

A continuous surjective map {{< m >}}p: \mathbb{R} \to S^1{{< /m >}} defined by {{< m >}}p(x) = e^{2\pi i x}{{< /m >}} is the **universal covering map**. The real line {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} is the **universal cover** of {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}}; it is simply connected (no holes), while {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} has fundamental group {{< m >}}\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}.

In pitch perception: the full pitch continuum {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} (ordered by frequency) is the universal cover of the chroma circle. Hearing a Shepard tone means perceiving only the chroma {{< m >}}\theta \in S^1{{< /m >}} without being able to lift to a specific height in {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}.
{{< /definition >}}

## Main Theorems

{{< theorem name="Shepard Tone Periodicity" label="Theorem 8.1" >}}
A Shepard scale ascending by semitones returns to the *exact same sound* after 12 steps. Formally: let {{< m >}}S_k{{< /m >}} denote the Shepard tone at chroma class {{< m >}}k \in \mathbb{Z}_{12}{{< /m >}}. Then {{< m >}}S_k = S_{k+12}{{< /m >}} as audio signals (the waveforms are identical), yet each step {{< m >}}S_k \to S_{k+1}{{< /m >}} contains only upward-moving partials.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
**Step 1 (Individual steps go up):** When chroma increases from {{< m >}}k{{< /m >}} to {{< m >}}k+1{{< /m >}}, every partial {{< m >}}\sin(2\pi f_n t){{< /m >}} in {{< m >}}S_k{{< /m >}} at frequency {{< m >}}f_n = f_\text{ref} \cdot 2^{n + k/12}{{< /m >}} maps to {{< m >}}\sin(2\pi f_n' t){{< /m >}} in {{< m >}}S_{k+1}{{< /m >}} where {{< m >}}f_n' = f_\text{ref} \cdot 2^{n + (k+1)/12} > f_n{{< /m >}}. Every partial rises.

**Step 2 (Global spectrum is unchanged):** The set of frequencies {{< m >}}\{f_\text{ref} \cdot 2^{n+k/12}\}_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}{{< /m >}} with the same envelope values {{< m >}}A(n){{< /m >}} is invariant under the shift {{< m >}}k \to k+12{{< /m >}} (equivalently {{< m >}}k/12 \to k/12 + 1{{< /m >}}), because this replaces {{< m >}}2^{n + k/12}{{< /m >}} by {{< m >}}2^{n+1+k/12}{{< /m >}} — a relabeling {{< m >}}n \mapsto n-1{{< /m >}} of the summation index, which preserves the sum (under appropriate convergence of the envelope).

**Step 3 (Paradox):** The listener hears individual partials rising (Step 1) but the global spectral envelope is unchanged (Step 2). The auditory system integrates the local motion cue (upward), producing the percept of continuous ascent, while lacking the information needed to track absolute height. After 12 steps, the identical signal repeats — the perception is of infinite ascent on a circle. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< theorem name="Tritone Paradox — Ambiguity from Antipodality" label="Theorem 8.2" >}}
Let {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\theta + 1/2 \in S^1{{< /m >}} be antipodal chromas. A Shepard tone pair {{< m >}}(S_\theta, S_{\theta+1/2}){{< /m >}} presented in sequence carries no objective information about direction (ascending vs. descending) because the clockwise and counterclockwise arcs on {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} between {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\theta + 1/2{{< /m >}} are equal in length.

Formally: any continuous "direction function" {{< m >}}d: S^1 \times S^1 \to \{+1,-1\}{{< /m >}} that is antisymmetric ({{< m >}}d(\theta, \phi) = -d(\phi, \theta){{< /m >}}) and depends only on the circular arc length must satisfy {{< m >}}d(\theta, \theta + 1/2) = 0{{< /m >}}, which is not in {{< m >}}\{+1,-1\}{{< /m >}} — i.e., no such function can be defined at antipodal pairs.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
The circular distance between antipodal points {{< m >}}\theta{{< /m >}} and {{< m >}}\theta + 1/2{{< /m >}} on {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} (normalized to circumference 1) is {{< m >}}1/2{{< /m >}} in both directions. An antisymmetric function {{< m >}}d{{< /m >}} satisfying {{< m >}}d(\theta,\phi) = \text{sgn}(\phi - \theta \bmod 1 - 1/2){{< /m >}} (i.e., {{< m >}}+1{{< /m >}} if the shorter clockwise arc is less than {{< m >}}1/2{{< /m >}}, {{< m >}}-1{{< /m >}} otherwise) is undefined (or must choose a convention) exactly when the arc lengths are equal, i.e., at separation {{< m >}}1/2{{< /m >}}. There is no canonical choice of {{< m >}}\pm 1{{< /m >}} at this degeneracy point. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}

**Experimental consequence (Deutsch 1986):** Since the sound itself is ambiguous, listeners' responses are determined by an internal prior — a learned "height template" mapping chroma to expected height. Deutsch found that this template correlates with the speaker's native language tonal range: speakers of tonal languages with high fundamental frequencies (e.g., Vietnamese) have templates peaked at different chroma values than speakers of non-tonal languages (e.g., California English). The template is calibrated in childhood by the statistical distribution of fundamental frequencies in ambient speech.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< theorem name="Chroma as a Quotient Group" label="Theorem 8.3" >}}
The chroma circle is the quotient group {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} (real numbers modulo 12, corresponding to semitones modulo octave equivalence). The natural projection {{< m >}}p: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} is a group homomorphism with kernel {{< m >}}12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}.

The pitch helix {{< m >}}S^1 \times \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} decomposes pitch into an element of the quotient group (chroma) and a coset representative (height). The Shepard construction projects out the coset representative, retaining only the quotient group element.
{{< /theorem >}}

{{< proof >}}
{{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} is an abelian group under addition. {{< m >}}12\mathbb{Z} = \{12k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}{{< /m >}} is a closed subgroup. The quotient group {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}/12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} has elements {{< m >}}[x] = x + 12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}, and inherits addition {{< m >}}[x] + [y] = [x+y]{{< /m >}}. Since {{< m >}}12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} is a discrete closed subgroup of {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}, the quotient is a Lie group homeomorphic to {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}}.

Any {{< m >}}x \in \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}} can be written uniquely as {{< m >}}x = q + r{{< /m >}} where {{< m >}}q \in 12\mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}} (the height component, selecting the octave) and {{< m >}}r = x \bmod 12 \in [0,12){{< /m >}} (the chroma component). The projection {{< m >}}p(x) = [x]{{< /m >}} forgets {{< m >}}q{{< /m >}} and retains {{< m >}}r{{< /m >}}. {{< m >}}\square{{< /m >}}
{{< /proof >}}

{{< proposition label="Prop 8.1" name="Shepard Tone and Ambiguous Fundamental" >}}
A Shepard tone is a signal with no single well-defined fundamental frequency. Every Shepard tone at chroma {{< m >}}k{{< /m >}} is a harmonic series on a *virtual* fundamental {{< m >}}f_0 = f_\text{ref} \cdot 2^{k/12}{{< /m >}}, but simultaneously also on all octave multiples {{< m >}}f_0 \cdot 2^n{{< /m >}} for {{< m >}}n \in \mathbb{Z}{{< /m >}}. The auditory system's pitch-extraction mechanism (which normally resolves fundamental frequency via harmonic template matching) receives equally strong evidence for every octave, and cannot resolve the ambiguity without additional height cues.
{{< /proposition >}}

## Musical Connection

{{< musical-connection >}}
**Bach's Canon per Tonos (1747) — a topological anticipation**

Bach's *Musical Offering* (BWV 1079) contains a modulating canon, *Canon per Tonos*, that ascends through all six whole-tone steps of the major scale before returning to the starting key — but one octave higher. Played in an infinite loop, it creates the same ascending-without-arriving sensation as a Shepard scale, 217 years before Shepard's experiment.

The mathematical structure is identical: the canon traverses the quotient space {{< m >}}\mathbb{Z}_6{{< /m >}} (six modulating steps) while the fundamental frequency shifts upward, making the "height" component grow without bound. The listener, if the performance starts and ends identically (which requires an implicit octave transposition), perceives a circular journey.

Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (1979) opens with this canon as an emblem of strange loops — self-referential structures that seem to ascend or descend while cycling back.

**Hans Zimmer's Dunkirk (2017) — three layered Shepard scales**

Christopher Nolan's *Dunkirk* uses Shepard tones composed by Hans Zimmer as a structural device: three interlocking Shepard scales at different tempos correspond to the film's three temporal scales (one hour, one day, one week). The perpetual ascending tension physically embodies the narrative's escalating dread. The mathematical structure (three independent loops in {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}}, perceived as a single climbing line) mirrors the film's three narrative threads that independently cycle while the audience experiences them as linearly converging.

**Spatial audio extension: spherical harmonics**

For 3D sound field representation (ambisonics), the two-dimensional {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} chroma circle generalizes to the sphere {{< m >}}S^2{{< /m >}}. The analog of Fourier modes on {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} are **spherical harmonics**:

{{< dm >}}Y_l^m(\theta, \phi) = \sqrt{\frac{(2l+1)}{4\pi}\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}} P_l^m(\cos\theta) e^{im\phi}{{< /dm >}}

where {{< m >}}P_l^m{{< /m >}} are associated Legendre polynomials, {{< m >}}l \ge 0{{< /m >}} is the degree (angular frequency), and {{< m >}}-l \le m \le l{{< /m >}} is the order. An order-{{< m >}}N{{< /m >}} ambisonics system uses all {{< m >}}(N+1)^2{{< /m >}} spherical harmonics up to degree {{< m >}}N{{< /m >}}, representing the 3D sound field as a finite spatial Fourier series on the sphere.
{{< /musical-connection >}}

## Limits and Open Questions

1. **Individual differences in the tritone paradox.** Deutsch's finding that native language predicts direction responses is robust across replication, but the mechanism (critical period learning of fundamental frequency statistics vs. genetic predisposition) is debated. A fully predictive mathematical model of the individual height template is an open problem in auditory neuroscience.

2. **Continuous Shepard tones and glissandi.** The discrete Shepard scale uses 12 steps. A continuous variant (Risset glissando, named after Jean-Claude Risset, 1969) uses a continuously varying chroma and produces a continuously ascending tone. The mathematical treatment requires the topology of path spaces on {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} — which paths lift to bounded paths in the universal cover {{< m >}}\mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}?

3. **Pitch perception in other species.** Shepard tone experiments have been conducted on macaques, rhesus monkeys, and some songbirds. Whether the tritone paradox (linguistically calibrated height template) generalizes to non-linguistic animals is an open question that would test the cultural vs. acoustic origin of the height prior.

4. **Higher-dimensional pitch spaces.** Tymoczko's voice-leading geometry and Balzano's (1980) group-theoretic model of tonal pitch space both propose spaces richer than {{< m >}}S^1 \times \mathbb{R}{{< /m >}}. Whether there is a single "correct" mathematical model of pitch perception space, or whether different tasks (melody recognition, harmony, voice-leading) engage different geometric representations, is an active research question.

5. **Persistent homology of pitch spaces.** Topological data analysis (TDA) can be applied to chroma features of audio to detect the {{< m >}}S^1{{< /m >}} topology of the pitch class distribution. Whether higher Betti numbers ({{< m >}}\beta_2, \beta_3, \ldots{{< /m >}}) appear in the pitch space of complex harmonies (chords, polyphony) is an open application of computational topology.
